## 1. Which first-round claims survive when the source is actually read?

### Takeaway
The first-round statements about Wronski, Giesen, the DLSS/FSR mip-bias guidance, the Öztireli & Gross abstract and the NVTT/Castaño position are confirmed at source, with two important corrections of emphasis: Wronski explicitly says that a *slightly sharpening* downsampling filter can be desirable for mipmaps, and Giesen calls hardware anisotropic filtering "pretty damn good". Everything that depended on ACM, IEEE, PubMed, Intel's article, Unreal or Unity documentation remains unverified.

## 2. Strongest evidence against and for "level-0 sharpening causes temporal instability"; is anything measured?

### Takeaway
Nothing opened in either round measures temporal instability of sharpened textures; both sides rest on filter theory, vendor guidance and practitioner demonstrations. The strongest counter-evidence is that expert practitioners consider GPU anisotropic filtering very good, recommend mild sharpening or sharpening kernels for mip chains to offset bilinear blur, and that upscaler vendors ship strongly negative LOD biases by default; the strongest supporting evidence is that the same sources name high-frequency texture content as the case where this breaks, and that the vendor defaults depend on a temporal accumulation pass.

### Cited Findings

**Against the first-round conclusion (sharpening is tolerated or beneficial)**

- BLOG OPINION (expert). Giesen 2011 on hardware AF: vendors have "been hacking at this problem for years, and by now both converged on something pretty damn good at reasonable hardware cost"; "as a graphics programmer, you just don't need to care about the underlying anisotropic filtering algorithm as long as it's not broken". In a comment reply he describes AF as "a typically good approximation of Heckberts Elliptical Weighted Averaging filter". No measurement. — [fgiesen.wordpress.com 2011/07/04](https://fgiesen.wordpress.com/2011/07/04/a-trip-through-the-graphics-pipeline-2011-part-4/)
- BLOG OPINION. - THEORY + images. Wronski 2021: the least-squares inverse of bilinear reconstruction is the 3-tap unsharp mask [-0.175, 1.35, -0.175]. — [bartwronski.com 2021/07/20](https://bartwronski.com/2021/07/20/processing-aware-image-filtering-compensating-for-the-upsampling/)

### Inferences
- Magnitude comparison (own calculation). Wronski's recommended mip sharpening filter [-0.175, 1.35, -0.175] has gain 1.0 at DC, 1.35 at a quarter of the sampling rate and 1.70 at Nyquist, per axis. A Gaussian unsharp mask with radius 0.5 texel and amount 0.5 to 1.0 has gain 1.36 to 1.71 at Nyquist and 1.13 to 1.27 at a quarter of the sampling rate (formula from the first-round note). So the "mild USM" under discussion sits at or below the strength that an expert proposes as a routine correction for bilinear blur. This is the strongest argument that the first-round conclusion overstated the risk for small radius and moderate amount.
- That argument transfers to level 0 only partly. Wronski's compensation is justified where the level is *magnified* by bilinear filtering (his case: a level upsampled by up to 2x). For level 0 that is the true-magnification zone close to the aircraft and the cross-range direction under AF. In the trilinear zone between LOD 0 and 1, and along the major axis under AF, level 0 is *minified*, and there the boosted band is exactly the band at or above screen Nyquist. Wronski's own answer to the motion question ("some of the boosted frequencies will alias") covers that case.
- The DLSS/FSR evidence shows that under-filtering by one to two octaves is shipped by default and tolerated, but both documents attach it to a temporal accumulation pass that averages jittered frames. Whether X-Plane 12's anti-aliasing mode in use provides such a pass was not established in this round (no Laminar source found). Without it, the vendor tolerance does not carry over; with it, a mild level-0 sharpen is a far smaller intervention than a −1 to −2 bias.
- Counter-evidence does not rescue the first-round point about the level 0/1 contrast step, but it reframes it: Castaño's "preserving energy across mip levels" and Wronski's sharpened downsamplers both argue that levels 1..n of a box chain are too soft relative to level 0. Sharpening only level 0 moves in the opposite direction to what these authors recommend (sharpen or better-filter the lower levels). No source says viewers object to a sharper level 0; the literature opened simply does not consider it.
- Net assessment of evidence quality: for "causes shimmer" there is consistent theory plus two vendors naming the content class (high-frequency texture) and one expert discouraging sharpened box mips; against it there is expert opinion that AF is very good and that mild sharpening is beneficial, plus anecdotal still-image reports. Neither side has a measurement. The question is only decidable by a motion test on the actual content.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed measurement of flicker or shimmer versus texture sharpening strength, mip filter or LOD bias was found; arxiv.org searches ("mip bias temporal aliasing", "texture LOD bias upscaling flicker", "anisotropic texture filtering hardware quality", "mipmap downsampling filter sharpening") returned nothing relevant.
- Mavridis & Papaioannou 2011, the one paper claimed to document temporal aliasing of hardware AF, could not be opened (ACM 403).
- Unreal's mip-sharpening defaults and any Epic statement on shimmer could not be opened; the claim that Unreal recommends Sharpen settings for colour textures stays unverified.
- X-Plane 12 sampler state, global LOD bias and whether a temporal AA pass exists: no source found on developer.x-plane.com.

## 3. Measured data on BC1 error vs. local contrast/variance, on masking of texture-compression artefacts, and on JPEG + sharpening

### Takeaway
No opened source measures BC1 error as a function of local contrast or sharpening, and the two papers that reportedly measure masking of texture-compression artefacts (Griffin & Olano) could not be opened, so the masking counter-argument remains snippet-level. What was confirmed at source is qualitative: BC1's weak spots are smooth gradients and flat blocks, encoder authors treat smooth blocks as the visually critical case, artefacts are most visible where no detail masks them, and the project's ISPC encoder has no perceptual weighting.

### Cited Findings
- CODE. Intel ISPC BC1 path: PCA axis from 4 power iterations on an unweighted covariance, endpoints on that axis, 1 refinement iteration; a comment in the source notes it "could be improved by refinement" for the alpha path. No perceptual weights, no smooth-block handling. — [kernel.ispc](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GameTechDev/ISPCTextureCompressor/master/ispc_texcomp/kernel.ispc)
- BLOG (expert). Castaño 2022: BC1 palette entries "are a convex combination of the 2 colors"; near-optimal encoding projects colours onto the best-fit line and solves least squares per index clustering; the result is "nearly optimal" only because endpoints are "clamped and quantized". Confirms the format mechanics behind the first-round "endpoint line" reasoning; contains no content-dependent error data. — [ludicon.com 2022/11](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/2022/11/bc1-compression-revisited/)
- BLOG (expert). Giesen 2021: slow greyscale gradients in BC1 "tend to oscillate between areas tinged green and areas tinged purple" (RGB565 has no exact greys); decoders may differ by up to ±3% relative, 1/255 absolute. — [fgiesen.wordpress.com 2021/10/04](https://fgiesen.wordpress.com/2021/10/04/gpu-bcn-decoding/)
- TOOL DOCUMENTATION. Geldreich, bc7enc_rdo README: the rate-distortion optimiser is "modified to favor MSE on very smooth blocks"; disabling smooth-block error scaling "can result in noticeable artifacts on smooth/flat blocks at higher lambdas". An encoder author's working assumption that artefacts are visible in smooth blocks and tolerated in busy ones; no psychophysical data. — [bc7enc_rdo README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/richgel999/bc7enc_rdo/master/README.md)
- Encyclopaedic. Wikipedia "Compression artifact": block discontinuities "are most visible in flat areas, where there is little detail to mask the effect." — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_artifact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_artifact)
- Encyclopaedic. Wikipedia "S3 Texture Compression": S3TC image-quality loss is "an effect which is minimized by the ability to increase texture resolutions while maintaining the same memory requirements"; BC1–5 "decode slightly differently among GPU vendors". — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S3_Texture_Compression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S3_Texture_Compression)
- VENDOR/DEVELOPER BLOG. Laminar Research (B. Supnik), "The case for DDS", 2007: compression artefacts matter "Any time a texture is magnified so large that small color changes between two pixels is noticeable. (For example, compressing the color washes used for the sky make obvious artifacts.)"; a commenter reports "even in DXT1, the compression artifacts are not a problem" for colour textures. "Revisiting Texture Compression", 2010: "If the image file contains details that don't compress well, they're going to get splatted and stay splatted." Opinion, no numbers. — [developer.x-plane.com 2007/08](https://developer.x-plane.com/2007/08/the-case-for-dds/), [developer.x-plane.com 2010/09](https://developer.x-plane.com/2010/09/revisiting-texture-compression/)
- MEASURED but not content-resolved. icbc README: average PSNR vs. time for five BC1 encoders on Kodim + Waterloo (chart image only). — [icbc README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/castano/icbc/master/README.md)
- JPEG. Wikipedia "JPEG": "JPEG typically achieves 10:1 compression with a loss in image quality that, while perceptible, is widely agreed upon to be acceptable"; a difference image at quality 50 shows "changes occurring near sharp edges and having a block-like shape"; the article's sample table attributes obvious "macroblocking" to the low-quality rows. No statement on visibility thresholds at quality 75–90 and nothing on sharpening after JPEG. — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG)
- Encyclopaedic. Wikipedia "Unsharp masking": USM "may create unwanted conspicuous edge effects or increase image noise"; mitigation is an edge mask so sharpening applies "only ... to desired regions". — [en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsharp_masking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsharp_masking)

### Inferences
- The first-round derivation "USM raises absolute BC1 error roughly in proportion to the contrast gain" remains an inference; no opened source confirms or refutes it. It is cheap to measure locally with the project's own encoder: encode unsharpened and sharpened level 0, report per-block RMSE against the respective input binned by block variance, and compare error-to-signal ratio rather than absolute error.
- The masking counter-argument is plausible and supported indirectly: encoder authors and the encyclopaedic text locate visible artefacts in smooth/flat blocks, where an unsharp mask changes almost nothing, and treat busy blocks as tolerant. Sharpening adds contrast mostly to already busy blocks, so the extra BC1 error lands where it is least visible. The decisive measured support (Griffin & Olano) is unverified, so this should be reported as probable, not established.
- Two caveats to the masking argument follow from the opened sources. (1) Giesen's green/purple gradient oscillation and Supnik's "color washes" example are flat-area artefacts; if the unsharp mask amplifies JPEG ripple or sensor noise in flat areas, blocks that were near-constant acquire a small colour range, which can either hide banding or turn it into visible coloured noise. (2) The ISPC kernel has no perceptual or smooth-block weighting and only one refinement pass, so it will not protect smooth blocks the way bc7enc's RDO heuristic does.
- For JPEG at provider quality (about 75–90) the opened material gives no threshold. The first-round statement "sharpening amplifies blocking/ringing" is not contradicted, and the counter-claim "blocking is below visibility at that quality, so mild USM is harmless" has no opened source either. This is undecided and must be checked on real tiles (blockiness metric on the 8-pixel grid before and after USM).

### Gaps
- **No solid study found on BC1 error versus local contrast, variance or sharpening.** None of the opened encoder write-ups breaks error down by block content.
- **Griffin & Olano 2014/2015 unread** (ACM 403, PubMed cookie wall, NIST/UMBC not allowed). Whether they show masking strong enough to hide BC1 error in high-contrast diffuse textures cannot be stated from an opened source.
- Intel's article (PSNR of the ISPC BC1 encoder against others) could not be opened; van Waveren's DXT papers likewise.
- No measured numbers on JPEG blocking visibility at quality 75–90 or on blockiness growth under unsharp masking were obtained; the first-round JPEG papers sit on hosts that are still blocked.
- No source on coincident JPEG 8x8 and BC1 4x4 grids, on BC artefacts across trilinear mip blends, or on texture-locked artefacts in motion; these remain theory only, as in the first round.
