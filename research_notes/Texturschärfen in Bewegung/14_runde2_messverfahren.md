## 1. Verification at source of the round-1 findings

### Takeaway
The round-1 descriptions hold, with four changes that matter: (a) ColorVideoVDP's own paper states it does **not** model eye motion and was trained only on XR display artefacts plus still images, with no rendering-aliasing data; (b) FovVideoVDP, not ColorVideoVDP, is the VDP that was calibrated on a blur-versus-temporal-aliasing (flicker) trade-off, but its code is CC BY-NC; (c) on Intel's rendering-artefact dataset ColorVideoVDP reaches PLCC 0.744, barely above PSNR at 0.697, against 0.871 for CGVQM-5; (d) a model that is specifically about mipmapped textures in motion exists (CaMoJAB, Jindal et al. 2021) and was missed in round 1.

#### ColorVideoVDP (Mantiuk, Hanji, Ashraf, Asano, Chapiro; SIGGRAPH 2024; arXiv 2401.11485)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Temporal channels: four channels in total, "two achromatic and two chromatic". "we assume two temporal achromatic channels"; "We assume just one temporal channel for the red-green and violet-yellow cardinal directions". The achromatic signal is split "into sustained (low-pass) and transient (band-pass) channels. The chromatic channels are low-pass filtered and become insensitive to high-frequency chromatic flicker." Filters come from castleCSF; "a filter support of 250 ms is sufficient". — [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML, Sec. 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485)
- [OPENED] MEASURED (dataset description). Training and testing data (Table 2): XR-DAVID (train and test, SDR video, 14 scenes, 336 conditions, 8 display artefacts); UPIQ (train and test, SDR/HDR **images**, 84 scenes, 4159 conditions, 34 distortion types); LIVE HDR (test only, 21 scenes, 210 conditions, H.265 and bicubic upscaling); LIVE VQA (test only, 10 scenes, 150 conditions, H.264, MPEG-2, transmission); KADID-10k (test only, images, 25 distortion types). — [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485)
- [OPENED] MEASURED. XR-DAVID's eight distortions, each at three strengths: spatiotemporal dithering, light source nonuniformity, blur (MTF degradation), reduced contrast, waveguide nonuniformity, dynamic correction error, colour fringes, chroma subsampling. 77 naive participants, side-by-side pairwise comparison, Eizo CG3146 at 300 cd/m2, 60 Hz, chin rest at 0.73 m, 77 pixels per degree. None of the eight is texture aliasing, mip shimmer, TAA or any rendering artefact. — [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML, Sec. 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Limitations, verbatim: "ColorVideoVDP lacks higher-level models of saliency or annoyance ... It was not trained to predict accurate spatial distortion maps ... (as no such data is available for video). ColorVideoVDP does not model the effect of glare ..., gaze-contingent vision (found in FovVideoVDP ...), eye motion (Laird et al., 2006; Denes et al., 2020), or binocular vision". On heatmaps: "the metric has not been trained to produce accurate heatmaps as there are no datasets that could be used for that purpose ... The heatmaps are meant to help interpret the single-valued JOD quality predictions." — [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Reported result: "a substantial gain in performance of ColorVideoVDP over the second-best metric — VMAF" (Fig. 16, RMSE in JOD). #### FovVideoVDP (Mantiuk, Denes, Chapiro, Kaplanyan, Rufo, Bachy, Lian, Patney; ACM TOG 40(4), SIGGRAPH 2021)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Two achromatic temporal channels, sustained and transient, the transient modelled "as the first derivative of the response of the sustained channel", peak responses at 0 Hz and 5 Hz. Luminance only; no colour. — [paper PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf)
- [OPENED] MEASURED (dataset description). The authors built **FovDots**, a dataset that isolates "velocity, contrast, luminance, and the tradeoff between blur and temporal noise (aliasing) due to low sampling rates". Stimulus: moving dots at 0, 2, 5 deg/s. Temporal accumulation I_t = beta I_R + (1 - beta) I_(t-1) "with amortized supersampling [Yang et al. 2009] with no reprojection"; "Low beta values result in blur, whereas high beta values result in temporal aliasing." 35 participants, HTC Vive Pro Eye at 90 fps, fixation on a central cross, 2-interval pairwise comparison. Other calibration data: UPIQ (images), DeepFovea and LIVE-FBT-FCVR (foveated video, mostly compression-like). — [paper PDF, Sec. 4 and 5.1](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM, and a statement against simple metrics on this artefact class: "most metrics excel in predicting compression distortions found in DeepFovea and LIVE-FBT-FCVR, but struggle with our FovDots dataset containing artifacts due to sampling and filtering." — [paper PDF, Sec. 5.3](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Limitations: no glare, no orientation-selective channels, no cross-channel masking, no colour, and "We do not model the loss of sensitivity due to eye movements as most datasets do not provide this information." — [paper PDF, Sec. 3](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf)
- [OPENED] Licence: the repository's LICENSE file is "Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International Public License"; PyPI metadata for `pyfvvdp` 1.2.2 says the same. **[CORRECTS ROUND 1]**, which left the licence open. — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gfxdisp/FovVideoVDP/main/LICENSE), [PyPI JSON](https://pypi.org/pypi/pyfvvdp/json)

#### Cambridge motion-quality models
- [ABSTRACT OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Denes, Jindal, Mikhailiuk, Mantiuk, "A perceptual model of motion quality for rendering with adaptive refresh-rate and resolution", SIGGRAPH 2020: predicts "the quality of motion given an object velocity and predictability of motion. The model considers two motion artifacts ...: non-smooth (juddery) motion, and blur. Blur is modeled as a combined effect of eye motion, finite refresh rate and display resolution." Fitted on experiments from 50 to 165 Hz. No aliasing or texture term. — [project page](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/motion_quality_model/)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Jindal, Wolski, Myszkowski, Mantiuk, "Perceptual Model for Adaptive Local Shading and Refresh Rate", ACM TOG 40(6), SIGGRAPH Asia 2021. Introduces CaMoJAB, a "Content-adaptive Metric of Judder, Aliasing and Blur". It models the texture pipeline explicitly: "The texture is first quantized and stored as a mipmap. Next, during the texture lookup, a mipmap level is selected according to the shading resolution ... If the object is in motion and is tracked by our gaze (smooth pursuit eye motion (SPEM)), it may introduce hold-type or eye motion blur." And: "Low shading resolution also results in the formation of aliases that distorts the lower frequencies. When the rendered image moves on a screen, our eyes undergo SPEM to follow it. This adds further blurring of the signal and may mask aliasing." Scope limits: "The following analysis models an image that is stabilized on the retina therefore we do not consider the changes of the signal over time"; box filter assumed for mip generation; "we assume isotropic filtering, but it is trivial to extend it to support anisotropic filtering"; "it does not model contrast masking ..., degradation of color, foveation ... or saliency"; conservative assumption "that the eye can follow movement in every VRS tile". — [paper PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/alsarr/Perceptual_Model_for_Adaptive_Local_Shading_and_Refresh_Rate.pdf)
- [OPENED] The ALSaRR repository ships CaMoJAB as MATLAB (`camojab.m`, `model_Dst.m`, prefitted parameters) and the experiment data: `vrs_exp_data.csv` (pairwise comparisons, Experiment 1), `vrs_exp_stimulus.mat` ("Textures used in the experiment"), plus Denes et al. 2020 data. Licence not read. — [ALSaRR README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gfxdisp/ALSaRR/main/README.md)
- [OPENED] Denes and Mantiuk, "Predicting visible flicker in temporally changing images" (HVEI 2020): parameters fitted to a flicker marking experiment (19 observers, 18 reference images, 3-fold cross-validation), aimed at display algorithms (black frame insertion, temporal resolution multiplexing). No packaged code found. — [paper PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/denes2020flicker_model.pdf)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE. ColorVideoVDP's subjective calibration contains no rendering aliasing at all; its temporal channel was fitted on XR display artefacts (dithering, per-frame eye-tracker error). Round 1's statement that it is "not shown to be validated on mip/texture shimmer" can be hardened to: its paper shows it was not.
- OWN INFERENCE. FovVideoVDP has the more relevant calibration for a blur-versus-flicker question (FovDots), although on moving dots under fixation in a headset, not textures on a monitor. The CGVQM table agrees on LIVE Flicker (0.942 against 0.852 for ColorVideoVDP). Its non-commercial licence is acceptable for a private benchmark but not for anything shipped.
- OWN INFERENCE. Denes et al. 2020 is not usable as a metric here: it predicts quality from velocity, refresh rate and resolution for an edge-like stimulus and has no texture or aliasing input. CaMoJAB is the closest published model to the actual question (mip level, alias energy, texture spectrum, velocity, pursuit blur) but it is an analytic model on a texture, not a video metric: it takes a texture and parameters, assumes box-filtered mips and isotropic filtering, and has no notion of a sharpening kernel or of BC1. It would need extending to answer the benchmark, but its structure (alias energy inside the CSF, masked by pursuit blur) is the right template for a home-made number.
- OWN INFERENCE. The motion-silencing abstracts support only a weak statement: silencing is peripheral and absent near fixation. For ground texture the pilot looks at, it gives no licence to discount shimmer.

## 2. Counter-evidence to the round-1 recommendation

Round-1 recommendation under test: "ColorVideoVDP as perceptual judge + analytically reprojected temporal error as flicker number + per-frame SSIM/PSNR/Butteraugli as control".

### Takeaway
The recommendation survives in structure but not in its choice of judge: on the only rendering-artefact benchmark, ColorVideoVDP is in the same band as PSNR and well below CGVQM, and on the only flicker dataset it is beaten by FovVideoVDP and by plain absolute error; more fundamentally it does not model eye motion, which is the dominant viewing condition for ground texture streaming past. No dataset with subjective scores for texture/mip shimmer was found, so every candidate is unvalidated for this task and the known-ordering sanity ladder is the deciding instrument, not any one metric.

This weakens round 1's argument that the ColorVideoVDP heatmap is the feature "most useful to this benchmark". ColorVideoVDP's own paper agrees the heatmaps are untrained. — [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML, Table 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546); [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. ColorVideoVDP "does not model ... eye motion (Laird et al., 2006; Denes et al., 2020)"; FovVideoVDP: "We do not model the loss of sensitivity due to eye movements". The Jindal et al. 2021 model for moving textures assumes the opposite condition, an image "stabilized on the retina" through smooth pursuit, and states that pursuit blur "may mask aliasing". — [arXiv 2401.11485 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11485); [FovVideoVDP PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf); [ALSaRR PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/alsarr/Perceptual_Model_for_Adaptive_Local_Shading_and_Refresh_Rate.pdf)

Caveats that limit this counter-evidence (all from the same paper):
- [OPENED] The comparison was run by CGVQM's authors; CGVQM was calibrated on the training split of CG-VQD while "None of the existing metrics were re-trained". The study was remote with uncontrolled viewing, and psychophysical metrics were configured to the experiment's viewing conditions "when available; otherwise, the 'standard-4K' configuration ... was applied", so the VDPs ran with a guessed display model on CG-VQD. CG-VQD clips are 3 s, 1024x1024, mostly 30 fps. — [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546)
- [OPENED] CGVQM itself "does not account for viewing conditions and display parameters", so it cannot express that distant-ground shimmer matters differently at 60 cm than at 2 m. — [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML, Sec. 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546)

#### Evidence that simple metrics do as well, or do not
- [OPENED] MEASURED. For: on LIVE Flicker mean absolute error (0.888) and GMSD (0.948) match or beat every perceptual video metric except FovVideoVDP; on GamingVideoSET mean absolute error (0.853 / 0.911) is within 0.03 of the best. — [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML, Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546)
- [OPENED] MEASURED and AUTHOR CLAIM. Against: on CG-VQD and NVS all simple metrics sit at 0.49 to 0.78; "video quality metrics generally outperform image-based quality metrics on these benchmarks, underscoring the importance of modeling temporal artifacts"; 3D-CNN features beat the 2D LPIPS by 23% PLCC on average. FovVideoVDP's authors report that most metrics "struggle with our FovDots dataset containing artifacts due to sampling and filtering". — [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546); [FovVideoVDP PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf)

#### Weaknesses of warping / reprojection error as a flicker measure
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Not zero for a perfect video: "due to the natural intensity difference between frames and the estimation error of optical flow, the WE is not zero even for ground-truth videos and hence it cannot reflect the temporal consistency accurately." The authors' fix, relational warping error (RWE), "subtracts the difference between adjacent ground-truth [frames], which considers the natural variations of video frames and the estimation error of optical flow". — Yue, Liang et al., "Toward Accurate and Temporally Consistent Video Restoration from Raw Data", [arXiv 2312.16247 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.16247)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Rewards blur: "The warping error of our method is a little higher than Deflicker, but it is closer to the ground truth. Through analysis, we find that warping error has inaccuracy in the estimation of optical flow, and this error is masked by image blurring in Deflicker." — "BlazeBVD", [arXiv 2403.06243 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06243)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. Same failure in a rendering-specific learned metric built on warped frame differences: "in cases where videos display good temporal stability but exhibit severe blurring, our method slightly overestimates the overall quality." The same paper warns the measure "shows varying sensitivity to different scene contents and motion velocities ... it is crucial that the scene contents and motion conditions of the compared videos are as consistent as possible." — Yang, Ji, Zhu, Yang, Jin, "No-Reference Rendered Video Quality Assessment: Dataset and Metrics", [arXiv 2510.13349 HTML, Sec. VII](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13349)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR PRACTICE. Disocclusion is handled by masking: the 2026 tessellation paper uses a "valid-warp mask" and reports the retained pixel fraction; ReVQ's stream marks "occluded areas" and removes them before differencing. — [arXiv 2602.23754 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23754); [arXiv 2510.13349 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13349)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR PRACTICE. Multi-interval differencing: ReVQ computes differences "for adjacent frames and for frames separated by one, two, and three intervals, enabling detection of flickering across various frequencies." — [arXiv 2510.13349 HTML, Sec. IV-C](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13349)
- No paper was found that proposes a **blur-normalised** temporal stability measure (flicker divided by, or conditioned on, retained sharpness). RWE normalises against the reference's own temporal change, not against blur.

#### Datasets with subjective scores for texture aliasing, mip bias or shimmer
- [OPENED] MEASURED. ReVQ-2k: 2,000 rendered videos from UE4/UE5, 15 scenes, 720p/1080p/2K at 60 Hz, 57,450 valid ratings from 16 retained annotators, with a separate temporal stability score (TS-MOS) beside overall quality. Varied settings: "view distances, anti-aliasing methods, post-processing effects, shadows, textures, effect quality, and resolution scaling", plus FSR, DLSS, TAAU. Texture quality is one of several randomly combined settings; texture filtering, mip bias and sharpening are not named or isolated. It is a no-reference dataset. Finding: "there are no data points in the quadrants for high OA-MOS and low TS-MOS". — [arXiv 2510.13349 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13349)
- [OPENED] The ALSaRR repository publishes pairwise-comparison data for textures moving at several velocities under variable-rate shading, which selects coarser mip levels and creates aliasing. This is the closest public subjective data to "mipmapped texture in motion" found. It varies shading rate, not mip filter or sharpening. — [ALSaRR README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/gfxdisp/ALSaRR/main/README.md); [ALSaRR PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/research/rainbow/projects/alsarr/Perceptual_Model_for_Adaptive_Local_Shading_and_Refresh_Rate.pdf)
- [OPENED] FovDots (blur versus temporal aliasing, moving dots, public at doi 10.17863/CAM.68683) and CG-VQD (rendering artefacts, public as a GitHub release) are the other two; neither contains texture filtering conditions. — [FovVideoVDP PDF](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/~rkm38/pdfs/mantiuk2021_VideoVDP.pdf); [CGVQM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/IntelLabs/CGVQM/main/README.md)
- arXiv searches for "texture aliasing subjective quality", "temporal flicker metric rendered video quality", "shimmer temporal aliasing perceptual", "flicker rendering perceptual metric" and "computer graphics video quality metric rendering artifacts" returned no dataset or metric specific to texture shimmer and no full-reference rendering metric newer than CGVQM. The arXiv search page matches all terms strictly, so this is a weak negative.

#### Full-reference with supersampled reference versus no-reference temporal-energy analysis
- [OPENED] MEASURED. Watson, Ahumada, Farrell, "The Window of Visibility: A Psychophysical Theory of Fidelity in Time-Sampled Visual Motion Displays", NASA Technical Paper 2211, 1983. The spectrum of a moving image lies on a line whose slope is set by velocity; sampling adds "parallel replicas at intervals of" the sampling frequency; "if these replicas lie outside the window of visibility, then the smoothly moving line and the sampled line will be indistinguishable." The critical sampling frequency "is [a] linear function of velocity, with an intercept given by the temporal frequency limit, and slope given by the spatial frequency limit"; for a band-limited stimulus it equals "the temporal frequency limit plus the velocity times the spatial frequency of the grating". Measured slopes were 6 and 13 cycles/deg for two observers. Observers "fixated a point at the center of the path of travel", so the theory as tested is for a fixating eye. The authors note that quantitative predictions would also need "the detailed shape of the window ..., the inhomogeneity of spatial sensitivity across the retina, and possible masking". — [NTRS 19830025267 PDF](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19830025267/downloads/19830025267.pdf)
- [ABSTRACT OPENED] The 1986 JOSA A version's NTRS abstract covers the same content. — [NTRS 19860047713](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19860047713)
- [OPENED] AUTHOR CLAIM. The no-reference rendered-video metric of Yang et al. needs motion estimation, takes "over ten seconds" per video, and is biased by content and velocity. Full-reference practice in CG-VQD uses a 16x SSAA reference. — [arXiv 2510.13349 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13349); [arXiv 2506.11546 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11546)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE. **Judge.** Demote ColorVideoVDP from "the perceptual judge" to one of three perceptual columns, and promote CGVQM from "second opinion" to equal standing: it is the only metric with subjective calibration on rendering aliasing and flicker, and it led on that data. Add FovVideoVDP as the third because of FovDots and LIVE Flicker. Where the three disagree on ordering, that is a result to report, not noise to average away. None of the three has seen a mip-filter or sharpening condition.
- OWN INFERENCE. **Eye motion is the largest unmodelled factor.** During a flight the viewer mostly tracks ground features. Neither VDP models pursuit. With a tracked texture, what reaches the retina is the texture stabilised, plus whatever changes along the trajectory (shimmer), plus hold-type blur from the display. A VDP fed raw screen frames sees a large temporal signal from the motion itself in both test and reference and applies a fixed-eye temporal CSF and masking to it. A practical workaround, unvalidated: also score a **pursuit-stabilised** version of the clip, i.e. reproject test and reference into a ground-fixed window (the benchmark knows the homography) so the texture is static and only shimmer and resampling change over time, then feed that to the video metrics. Report both the raw and the stabilised score. This is my construction; no paper read does it for a VDP, though CaMoJAB's "stabilized on the retina" analysis is the same idea in analytic form.
- OWN INFERENCE. **Reprojected temporal error: keep, in the relational form.** The published objections are (1) flow error, (2) non-zero for ground truth, (3) rewards blur, (4) disocclusion. In this benchmark (1) and (4) vanish (analytic homography, a plane has no disocclusion, only frame-edge entry, handled by a valid mask). (2) is answered by RWE-style subtraction of the reference's own temporal change, which round 1 already proposed. (3) remains and no published normalisation was found. One new weakness specific to analytic reprojection: sampling the rendered frame at sub-pixel reprojected positions needs an interpolation kernel, which low-passes sharp variants more than soft ones and so adds a resampling error that correlates with sharpness. Mitigations: compare only at integer-pixel correspondences where the trajectory lands near pixel centres, or apply the identical resampling to the reference and use the relational form, or render the stabilised window directly.
- OWN INFERENCE. **Blur control must be explicit.** Because every temporal-difference measure read is fooled by blur (three independent author statements), the flicker number should never be reported alone. Report it as a 2-D point against a sharpness/fidelity number and include a deliberately blurred variant (LOD bias +1) so the reader can see where "stable because blurred" lands.
- OWN INFERENCE. **Full-reference is still the right primary design, but add a no-reference spectral check.** Window-of-visibility reasoning says shimmer is visible when alias energy falls inside the visible spatio-temporal band, and gives a cheap no-reference diagnostic: along each ground trajectory, measure temporal-frequency energy that the supersampled reference does not have. That is the same quantity as the relational temporal error seen in the frequency domain, so it needs the reference anyway to separate legitimate change from aliasing. The fixation condition of the 1983 experiment means the window's numbers cannot be applied directly to a tracked ground texture. A pure no-reference learned metric (ReVQ-style) is not preferable: it needs flow, is content- and velocity-biased by its authors' own account, and cannot say whether sharpening moved the image toward or away from the truth.
- OWN INFERENCE. **Per-frame control metrics stay**, but the CGVQM table suggests swapping or adding GMSD/HaarPSI-type gradient metrics: they were the best simple metrics on both the rendering and the flicker dataset, ahead of SSIM and PSNR.
- OWN INFERENCE. **The sanity ladder becomes mandatory, not optional.** Round 1 suggested LOD bias -1 and +1 as known-ordering probes. Given that no metric is validated for this artefact, extend it: LOD bias -1, -0.5, 0, +0.5, +1, plus nearest-mip (no trilinear) and anisotropy off, and accept a metric for the sharpening comparison only if it ranks shimmer monotonically along the negative-bias leg and does not rank the +1 blur leg as best overall. A metric that fails the ladder is dropped from the report.
- OWN INFERENCE. **A small in-house viewing check is cheap and is the only real validation available.** Since no subjective dataset exists for this artefact, a two-alternative forced choice between a handful of variant pairs (even with one or two viewers at the real cockpit distance) would tell more about which metric to trust than any further literature.

### Gaps
- No subjective dataset isolating texture filtering, mip bias, mip filter kernel or texture sharpening under motion was found. State as "not found".
- No published blur-normalised temporal stability measure was found.
- No experiment was found that tests any VDP on pursuit-stabilised input, so the stabilisation workaround is untested.
- Whether the VDPs would close the gap to CGVQM on CG-VQD with a correct display model is unknown; the dataset's remote protocol makes that untestable.
- ReVQ-2k's public availability, venue and whether its metric code is released were not verified (the paper refers to a "project homepage" not opened).
- Later window-of-visibility work (Watson 2013 on high frame rates) was not opened.

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE. Display model to write for the benchmark: one custom JSON entry with the real monitor resolution, diagonal and viewing distance (cockpit distance, roughly 0.6 to 0.7 m), and `--fps 60`. Use the same entry for `cvvdp` and `fvvdp` (same scheme). Record the pixels-per-degree line each tool prints.
- OWN INFERENCE. Clip length: both VDP temporal filters span about 250 ms, and CGVQM works on clips of `min(fps, 30)` frames; CG-VQD used 3 s clips. Two to four seconds at 60 Hz is consistent with all three and keeps CPU runs feasible.
- OWN INFERENCE. CGVQM needs video files, so the benchmark must encode its PNG frames losslessly (for example FFV1 or lossless H.264 RGB) to avoid adding codec artefacts on top of the texture differences; whether `load_resize_vids` accepts every lossless codec was not checked. CGVQM was trained at 1024x1024 with no display model, so render the benchmark clip at a resolution whose pixels-per-degree at the real viewing distance is similar to a desktop monitor, and do not compare CGVQM scores across resolutions.
- OWN INFERENCE. CGVQM's max-over-patches default suits this benchmark: shimmer concentrates in the far-distance band, and worst-patch pooling will report that band rather than dilute it. Also log the per-patch values to get the distance profile.
- OWN INFERENCE. Licence check for the repo: MIT (cvvdp), Apache-2.0 (CGVQM), BSD (FLIP, VMAF) can sit in a dev-only benchmark script without issue; FovVideoVDP's non-commercial terms mean it should stay an optional, locally installed tool and not a dependency of anything distributed.
