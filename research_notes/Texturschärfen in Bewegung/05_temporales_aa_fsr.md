## 1. What anti-aliasing, upscaling and sharpening does X-Plane 12 actually offer?

### Takeaway
As far as could be established without opening the primary pages: X-Plane 12 offers MSAA (2x–8x) and FXAA in its deferred renderer plus AMD FSR as a spatial upscaler; Laminar's own blog is reported to say "MSAA + FSR work just fine, no TAA requirement", which implies the integrated FSR is the spatial FSR 1 family (FSR 2/3 require jitter, motion vectors and history). No primary source confirming a shipped TAA, DLSS, a user sharpening slider, the RCAS strength, or a texture LOD bias was found — all of these remain unverified.

### Inferences
- The Laminar statement "MSAA + FSR ... no TAA requirement" (snippet) is only coherent for a **spatial** upscaler. FSR 2 and FSR 3 upscaling are temporal: AMD's docs require jitter on all rendering, motion vectors and a history buffer (FSR 2.2.1 README; FSR 3.1.4 docs, both read). So the FSR in X-Plane 12 is, by this logic, FSR 1.x (EASU, optionally + RCAS). This is an inference, not a Laminar statement that I read.
- If X-Plane's FSR is FSR 1, then **no temporal accumulation exists in the X-Plane 12 pipeline** as far as found: no history resampling blur, no neighbourhood clamping, no TAA ghosting. Texture shimmer in motion is then governed only by mip selection, anisotropic filtering and the texture's own frequency content (see section 2), and the TAA literature (section 3) matters only for a possible future X-Plane TAA.
- AMD's FSR 1 integration guide (read) asks for a negative mip bias and an optional RCAS pass. Whether Laminar followed either recommendation is unknown. The Laminar remark that FSR gives "a less detailed image on purpose just like reducing resolution does" (snippet) is at least compatible with no compensating LOD bias, but does not prove it.
- A Texture Quality setting below maximum, or VRAM-pressure paging, means mip level 0 of an orthophoto may never be uploaded. A sharpening applied only to level 0 would then be invisible, and levels 1+ (generated by XEarthLayer from the unsharpened or sharpened source, depending on pipeline order) would be what is displayed. This follows from the third-party description only.

The claim "X-Plane 12 has no TAA" is therefore supported only by one third-party guide and by the absence of contrary primary evidence, and contradicted by one unverified forum line.

## 2. MSAA and FXAA: do they touch texture interiors?

### Takeaway
No source on this question could be opened, so nothing is cited; the standard position (MSAA shades once per pixel and anti-aliases only geometric coverage; FXAA is a post-process luma-edge filter) is textbook knowledge that the report writer must source independently. The one relevant thing read is AMD's remark that MSAA-resolved gradients needed special handling in RCAS.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_fsr1.h` (2021), RCAS section: the naive clip-solving sharpening "has issues with MSAA input as the steps along the gradient cause edge detection issues. So RCAS uses 4x the maximum and 4x the minimum ... in place of the individual taps ... This stabilizes RCAS." — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4, FSR 1.2 "Expected input": "Image should already be well anti-aliased by a technique like TAA, MSAA etc." MSAA is explicitly accepted as the AA feeding FSR 1. — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-spatial.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-spatial.md)

### Inferences
- Under MSAA (unverified textbook reasoning, not from an opened source): the fragment shader, and therefore the texture fetch, runs once per pixel; only coverage/depth are multisampled. Ground-texture interiors of a terrain mesh are thus not anti-aliased by MSAA at all. Their only band-limiting is trilinear/anisotropic mip filtering. A sharpened level 0 raises energy near the texture's Nyquist limit, and at exactly the distances where level 0 is selected at roughly 1 texel per pixel this is the content most prone to shimmer under sub-pixel camera motion.
- FXAA (unverified reasoning): a luma-contrast edge detector plus directional blur in screen space. It cannot distinguish a geometric edge from a high-contrast texture edge, so unsharp-mask halos in the orthophoto (roof edges, road markings) raise local contrast above FXAA's edge threshold more often, and FXAA will then blur exactly the detail the sharpening created. Net effect: partial cancellation plus FXAA's own temporal instability on sub-pixel features. No measurement found.
- With "MSAA + FXAA" both statements apply at once; neither supplies temporal stability for texture interiors.

### Gaps
- Not opened, so not cited: Lottes, "FXAA" white paper (NVIDIA, 2009/2011); Reshetov, "Morphological Antialiasing" (HPG 2009); Jimenez et al., "SMAA: Enhanced Subpixel Morphological Antialiasing" (Eurographics 2012); Akenine-Möller et al., *Real-Time Rendering* 4th ed. (2018), ch. 5. The two inferences above need these as sources.
- No study found (in the searches run) measuring texture-interior shimmer of sharpened vs. unsharpened textures under MSAA/FXAA.

## 3. TAA: blur, negative mip bias, flicker, clamping failure, post-sharpening

### Takeaway
None of the TAA primary literature could be opened, so no claim is cited from it. What was read are the vendor documents of the two temporal upscalers, and they agree on the core trade-off: negative mip bias restores texture detail but high-frequency texture content then shows temporal aliasing (flicker, moiré), and both vendors tell developers to reduce the bias for exactly that content.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD, FSR 2.2.1 README (2022–2023), "Mipmap biasing": "Applying a negative mipmap biasing will typically generate an upscaled image with better texture detail. We recommend ... `mipBias = log2(renderResolution/displayResolution) - 1.0;`" followed by: "It is suggested that applications adjust the MIP bias for specific high-frequency texture content which is susceptible to showing temporal aliasing issues." — [FidelityFX-FSR2 README](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR2/blob/master/README.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD, FSR 3.1.4 upscaler docs (FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4): identical formula and identical caveat sentence. — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-upscaler.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — NVIDIA, "DLSS Super Resolution Programming Guide", rev. 310.6.0, March 2026, §3.5: the engine "must set the mip-map bias ... to a value lower than 0. This improves overall image quality as textures are sampled at the display resolution rather than the lower render resolution". Formula: `DlssMipLevelBias = NativeBias + log2(Render XResolution / Display XResolution) - 1.0 + epsilon` (−2.0 for Performance mode, ratio 0.5). — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same guide, §3.5: "Due to the temporal nature of DLSS, applying the recommended negative LOD Bias to the input can sometimes lead to increased temporal stability [sic — context shows instability is meant], in the form of flickering and/or moiré. This will be highly dependent on the scene content such as texture detail." Remedy: make the bias less aggressive on the problematic content, "without going above log2(Render XResolution / Display XResolution)"; "this is going to be a tradeoff between preservation of detail and aliasing." — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same guide, §3.5.1 "Mip-Map Bias Caveat: High-Frequency Textures": "If the mip levels are biased on textures with high frequency patterns, this can lead to artifacts when DLSS tries to reconstruct the full resolution frame." Example given: LED-screen / stock-ticker textures; advice: leave the bias at default for that material. — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same guide, §3.5 note: if a negative bias is already applied at native resolution, "some art assets may have been tuned for the default bias", and the result with DLSS can be too large or too small. Evidence that texture sharpness and mip bias are treated as a jointly tuned pair. — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD, FSR 3.1.4 docs, "Compute Luminance Instability": "Luminance instability is a measure if the brightness of an input pixel would be perceived as flickering. If this is the case, color clamping will be reduced in the accumulation pass, since otherwise the history would always be clamped to the current luminance, which then would lead to more flickering in the final output image." This is a vendor-documented statement of the clamping-versus-flicker trade-off: strict neighbourhood clamping on flickering high-contrast input reproduces the flicker; relaxing it trades flicker for more history (ghosting risk). — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-upscaler.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-upscaler.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD, FSR 2.2.1 README: a neighbourhood luminance check detects "thin features" which are then locked to preserve them; for the auto-generated composition mask, a too-small threshold "will cause visual instability. Larger values can cause ghosting." — [FidelityFX-FSR2 README](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR2/blob/master/README.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD, FSR 2.2.1 README and FSR 3.1.4 docs: temporal upscaling is followed by an RCAS stage at presentation resolution; quick-start checklist item 15: "Applications should also expose a sharpening slider to allow end users to achieve additional quality." Post-TAA/upscale sharpening is thus a standard, user-controllable stage in the vendor pipeline. — [FidelityFX-FSR2 README](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR2/blob/master/README.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — NVIDIA DLSS guide §3.11 and revision history: DLSS's own sharpening pass is deprecated (reference removed in rev. 2.3.2, 03/08/2022); developers wanting more sharpness are pointed to NVIDIA Image Scaling and are "highly recommended" to expose an end-user slider. — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)

### Inferences
- A pre-sharpened mip 0 is, in spectral terms, the same kind of intervention as a negative mip bias: both raise the high-frequency energy delivered per screen pixel. The vendor caveats on negative bias ("flickering and/or moiré", "temporal aliasing issues" for high-frequency texture content) therefore transfer qualitatively to pre-sharpened textures under any temporal technique. The two effects stack: sharpened mip 0 plus the recommended −1 extra bias is the worst case the DLSS guide's §3.5.1 warns about.
- Under a temporal technique the visible result of extra texture contrast depends on which side of the clamping trade-off the content lands: where history is rejected (clamped to the current neighbourhood) the raw per-frame aliasing shows as flicker; where history is kept, sub-pixel detail is averaged away (blur) and the offline sharpening is partly undone. Either way the sharpening does not arrive at the eye as intended while the camera moves.
- Since X-Plane 12 appears to have no TAA (section 1), these effects are hypothetical for X-Plane today. They would become relevant if Laminar ships TAA/temporal upscaling, and they are relevant now for any user forcing driver-level temporal features, which no source documented.

### Gaps
- Not opened, hence not cited; the report writer must verify these before attributing anything to them: Karis, "High Quality Temporal Supersampling" (SIGGRAPH 2014 Advances course); Yang, Liu & Salvi, "A Survey of Temporal Antialiasing Techniques", CGF 39(2):607–621, 2020, DOI 10.1111/cgf.14018 (bibliographic data confirmed via publisher listing in search results only; [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cgf.14018), full text reported at [Eurographics DL](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1111/cgf14018)); Pedersen, "Temporal Reprojection Anti-Aliasing in INSIDE" (GDC 2016); Salvi, "An Excursion in Temporal Supersampling" (GDC 2016); Jimenez, "Filmic SMAA" (SIGGRAPH 2016) and "Dynamic Temporal Antialiasing ... in Call of Duty" (SIGGRAPH 2017).
- Unverified recollection (from training knowledge, not from reading in this session — do not cite without checking): Karis 2014 is commonly credited with describing blur from repeated bilinear history resampling, neighbourhood clamping/clipping in YCoCg, flicker on high-contrast sub-pixel features, and a post-TAA sharpening filter; the Yang et al. survey is commonly cited for a taxonomy of blurriness, ghosting and temporal instability and for the mip-bias discussion under temporal upsampling. Page-level confirmation is required.
- No study was found that measures flicker or ghosting as a function of texture pre-sharpening strength under TAA.

## 4. CAS and RCAS: what do they do with input that already contains sharpening overshoot?

### Takeaway
Both filters only protect against clipping at the signal limits 0 and 1; neither detects or discounts existing overshoot in the mid-tones, so pre-sharpened halos that are not near black or white are sharpened again at close to full strength. RCAS additionally caps its negative lobe at 0.1875 and has an optional noise detector, off by default, that at most halves sharpening on isolated-pixel detail. No source documenting "double sharpening" of pre-sharpened textures was found.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_cas.h` v1.20190610 (2019): "CAS is a spatial only filter. CAS enchances sharpness and local high-frequency contrast, and with or without added upsampling." It uses a 3x3 window; "The filter kernel adapts to local contrast (adjusting the negative lobe strength of the filter kernel)." — [FidelityFX-CAS `ffx_cas.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/blob/master/ffx-cas/ffx_cas.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file, the adaptation rule: a soft min and max of the neighbourhood are computed; "This minimum distance to the signal limit is divided by MAX to get a base sharpening amount 'A'"; "'A' ... is shaped with a sqrt(). This 'A' ranges from 0 := no sharpening, to 1 := full sharpening. Then 'A' is scaled by the sharpness knob while being transformed to a negative lobe (values from -1/5 to -1/8 for A=1)." The sharpness knob is documented as "0 := default (lower ringing), 1 := maximum (higest ringing)". — [FidelityFX-CAS `ffx_cas.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/blob/master/ffx-cas/ffx_cas.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file, artefact notes: "It is likely better to reduce the amount of film grain which happens before CAS (as CAS will amplify grain)"; "Running CAS on perceptual inputs will yield over-sharpening"; with PQ-encoded data "the positive side of the ring created by the negative lobe tends to become over-bright". AMD itself documents ringing, grain amplification and over-sharpening as failure modes. — [FidelityFX-CAS `ffx_cas.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/blob/master/ffx-cas/ffx_cas.h)
- VENDOR CLAIM — AMD CAS README v1.0 (2020) and FidelityFX SDK CAS 1.2 doc: CAS "was created to provide natural sharpness without artifacts". Marketing-level statement, in tension with the header's own caveats above. — [FidelityFX-CAS README](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/blob/master/README.md); [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `contrast-adaptive-sharpening.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/contrast-adaptive-sharpening.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_fsr1.h` (2021), RCAS: "CAS uses a simplified mechanism to convert local contrast into a variable amount of sharpness. RCAS uses a more exact mechanism, solving for the maximum local sharpness possible before clipping. RCAS also has a built in process to limit sharpening of what it detects as possible noise." 5-tap cross; `output = (w*(n+e+w+s)+m)/(4*w+1)`; "RCAS solves for 'w' by seeing where the signal might clip out of the {0 to 1} input range ... Then chooses the 'w' which results in no clipping, limits 'w', and multiplies by the 'sharp' amount." — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file: the hard limiter is `#define FSR_RCAS_LIMIT (0.25-(1.0/16.0))` (= 0.1875), commented "This is set at the limit of providing unnatural results for sharpening." The sharpness parameter is in stops: "{0.0 := maximum sharpness, to N>0, where N is the number of stops (halving) of the reduction of sharpness}" (code: `sharpness=exp2(-sharpness)`). — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file, noise handling: "RCAS does a simple highpass which is normalized against the local contrast then shaped ... This is used as a noise detection filter, to reduce the effect of RCAS on grain, and focus on real edges." It is compiled in only with `#define FSR_RCAS_DENOISE 1`, described as "a more expensive path to avoid some sharpening of noise"; AMD suggests applying film grain after RCAS rather than enabling it. In code the factor is `nz = 1 - 0.5*saturate(|highpass| / (max - min))`, i.e. it reduces the lobe by at most 50 %. — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4, FSR 1.2: RCAS is optional per integration ("If RCAS sharpening is required, add the FFX_FSR1_ENABLE_RCAS flag"; dispatch fields `enableSharpening`, `sharpness`); "A negative input to RCAS will output NaN!". So an FSR 1 integration can ship with or without RCAS, at any strength. — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-spatial.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-spatial.md)

### Inferences
- **The adaptivity is about headroom to 0/1, not about "already sharp".** In CAS, `A = sqrt(min(MIN, 1−MAX)/MAX)`; in RCAS the lobe is bounded by `min(mn,e)/(4·mx)` and `(1−max(mx,e))/(4·mn−4)`. Both shrink only when the local neighbourhood approaches black or white. A pre-sharpened halo in mid-tones (for example a neighbourhood spanning 0.3–0.6) leaves ample headroom, so the limiter does not engage and the halo is amplified a second time. The popular reading "CAS reduces sharpening where contrast is already high" holds only where high contrast means near-saturated values. (Inference from the formulas read; no AMD statement says this explicitly.)
- **Where the limiter does engage, the first-pass damage is already done.** Unsharp masking of the texture offline clips at 0/255 in the texture itself (and BC1 quantises it further). RCAS then sees values at the signal limit and applies little sharpening there, so RCAS will not worsen clipped highlights much, but it cannot restore them.
- **Pre-sharpened single-texel detail resembles RCAS's definition of noise** (a centre pixel deviating from its cross neighbours by a large share of the local range). With `FSR_RCAS_DENOISE` off (the default) it is sharpened fully; with it on, at most halved. Sensor noise and JPEG artefacts in satellite imagery, amplified once by offline unsharp masking, are thus amplified again. AMD's own statement that "CAS will amplify grain" supports the direction of this inference.
- **Worst-case gain can be bounded from the kernel.** With RCAS at maximum (`w = −0.1875`) the output is `(m − 0.1875·Σ)/0.25 = 4m − 0.75·Σ`, i.e. a centre-to-neighbour-mean difference is amplified 4x for an isolated pixel, before the anti-clip solve reduces it. Stacked on an offline unsharp mask this is a multiplicative, not additive, high-frequency boost. (Arithmetic on the documented formula; not a measured result.)
- All of this applies to X-Plane only if its FSR path runs RCAS, which is unverified (section 1), and only when the user enables FSR. With FSR off, no evidence was found that X-Plane applies any post-sharpening.

### Gaps

## 5. FSR 1 / EASU specifics: high-frequency and aliased input, and the effect of render scale on mip selection

### Takeaway
EASU is a single-pass, edge-adaptive Lanczos-type spatial upscaler that clamps its output to the nearest 2x2 input neighbourhood; it expects well anti-aliased, noise-free input rendered with a negative mip bias. Rendering below display resolution without that bias shifts texture sampling to coarser mips, so level 0 is used less and a level-0-only sharpening loses reach.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_fsr1.h` (2021): "EASU provides a high quality spatial-only scaling at relatively low cost ... Quality from 1x to 4x area scaling is good." "EASU runs in a single pass, so it applies a directionally and anisotropically adaptive radial lanczos." — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file: "The lanzcos filter has negative lobes, so by itself it will introduce ringing. To remove all ringing, the algorithm uses the nearest 2x2 input texels as a neighborhood, and limits output to the minimum and maximum of that neighborhood." — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same file, input requirements: "There should be no banding in the input. There should be no high amplitude noise in the input. There should be no noise in the input that is not at input pixel granularity." Apply "at the end of the frame after tonemapping but before film grain or composite of the UI". — [FidelityFX-FSR `ffx_fsr1.h`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/blob/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — AMD FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4, FSR 1.2 "Expected input": "Image should already be well anti-aliased by a technique like TAA, MSAA etc."; "Image should be generated using negative MIP bias to increase texture detail."; "Image should be noise free." — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-spatial.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-spatial.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — same document: FSR 1 "is a spatial upscaler: it works by taking the current anti-aliased frame at render resolution and upscaling it to display resolution without relying on other data such as frame history or motion vectors." EASU "detects gradient reversals ... The intensity of the gradient reversals defines the weights to apply to the reconstructed pixels"; RCAS "extracts pixel detail in the upscaled image". — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-spatial.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-spatial.md)
- VENDOR CLAIM — same document: "FSR1 provides consistent upscaling quality regardless of whether the frame is in movement, which can provide quality advantages compared to other types of upscalers." (True in the sense that it adds no temporal artefacts; it also removes none.) — [FidelityFX-SDK v1.1.4 `super-resolution-spatial.md`](https://github.com/GPUOpen-LibrariesAndSDKs/FidelityFX-SDK/blob/v1.1.4/docs/techniques/super-resolution-spatial.md)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION — NVIDIA DLSS guide §3.5 (general statement about any reduced-resolution rendering): a negative bias makes textures "sampled at the display resolution rather than the lower render resolution". — [NVIDIA/DLSS programming guide PDF](https://github.com/NVIDIA/DLSS/blob/main/doc/DLSS_Programming_Guide_Release.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Mip shift from render scale.** Hardware LOD is computed from screen-space derivatives at render resolution. Rendering at 1/s of display resolution per axis without a bias raises the selected LOD by log2(s): +0.58 at 1.5x, +1.0 at 2.0x per-axis scale. (The FSR 1 preset factors 1.3x / 1.5x / 1.7x / 2.0x are from memory and were not confirmed in the files read.) Level 0 is then reached only at correspondingly shorter distances, shrinking the screen area in which a sharpened level 0 is ever displayed. If X-Plane applies AMD's recommended negative bias this shift is cancelled, at the price of under-filtered texture sampling at render resolution, i.e. more per-frame texture aliasing going into EASU.
- **EASU preserves pre-existing ringing.** The 2x2 min/max clamp prevents EASU from *creating* overshoot, but overshoot already present in the rendered frame (unsharp-mask halos from the texture) lies inside the min/max and is carried through and enlarged with the image. EASU then treats strong halos as edges ("gradient reversals"), and RCAS after it sharpens them again (section 4).
- **Aliased or noisy input is outside EASU's stated operating conditions.** A spatial upscaler has no means to distinguish texture aliasing from signal; shimmer present at render resolution is upscaled, and with RCAS amplified. AMD's "no high amplitude noise" and "well anti-aliased" requirements are the documented form of this limitation. Since MSAA does not anti-alias texture interiors (section 2), a sharpened orthophoto under MSAA + FSR 1 is the configuration in which the input is least compliant with these requirements.
- **No temporal filter means no hiding place.** Without accumulation, any frame-to-frame variation from sub-texel motion over high-contrast texels reaches the display unattenuated. Pre-sharpening raises the amplitude of that variation roughly in proportion to the high-frequency gain applied.

### Gaps
- Whether X-Plane 12 applies a negative LOD bias under FSR is unknown (see section 1).
- FSR 1 quality-mode scale factors and Laminar's mapping of its FSR slider positions ("Ultra", etc.) to them were not verified.
- The original FSR 1.0 repository README (read) contains no integration guidance; the "expected input" list comes from the later FidelityFX SDK v1.1.4 documentation of FSR 1.2.
