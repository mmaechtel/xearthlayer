## 1. X-Plane 12: which anti-aliasing, upscaling, sharpening and texture filtering does it actually use?

### Takeaway
Confirmed from Laminar's own words: X-Plane 12 has MSAA (new in 12, in the deferred renderer) and FXAA, an FSR upscaling slider that is the spatial FSR 1 ("we use FSR and not FSR2 or DLSS") because the engine has no motion vectors, and no TAA. RCAS was announced as a 12.1.0 graphics feature (February 2024) and a user comment of September 2024 refers to a persistent "rcas option", but no Laminar text describing its strength, default, user control, or whether it runs without FSR could be read, because the release notes are on a blocked host.

**Detail textures ("decals") and Laminar's rationale**
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Laminar article "Using Decals to Add Detail To Scenery", 13 May 2014: "Decals are overlay textures that sit on top of a regular albedo (day-time) texture to add high-frequency, high-res detail to an existing texture." "Decals contain high frequency pixels at high res; when viewed from far away, the entire decal blends together into a constant color/grayscale. The decal shader ensures that when a decal has become a solid tone (because it is far away) the net result is the same as your underlying albedo. In other words, as you move away from the decal, the decal blurs into 'no effect'." Decals are allowed in `.ter`, `.pol` and `.net` files. — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, "Webservers, Documentation, and Terrible Titles", 18 July 2024: detail textures "let us add fine detail at high resolution without using a ton of extra VRAM efficiently. We use detail textures with bits of 'gravel' to make the runways look more realistic. We introduced detail textures over a decade ago in X-Plane 10 to enhance ground detail around airports and in the new (at the time) autogen cities." Laminar now calls them "detail textures", not "decals". — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/07/webservers-documentation-and-terrible-titles/)
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, 24 February 2024 (12.1.0): "The material system now features normal-map decals. These decals add high-frequency 'texture' to a surface by perturbing the normals (direction of light bounce) instead of just 'painting' them." — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/02/a-few-notes-on-12-1-0-for-developers/)

### Inferences
- **Laminar's own solution to "ground texture too soft up close" is added high-frequency detail that fades out with distance, not sharpening of the base texture.** The decal shader is explicitly built so that the detail "blurs into 'no effect'" far away. That is the property an offline unsharp mask on mip 0 lacks only partly: a mip-0-only sharpening also disappears at distance (through mip selection), but it amplifies what is already in the image (including noise and compression artefacts) instead of adding new band-limited detail. OWN INFERENCE.
- **A sharpened level 0 is shown only when three conditions hold at once:** the texture slider is at maximum, the VRAM pager has not reduced the tile ("cuts down the top res"), and the tile is close enough to the aircraft for full resolution (`LOAD_CENTER` behaviour). With VRAM pressure still a live issue in 12.4.4, this cannot be assumed for all users. OWN INFERENCE from the Laminar statements above.
- **If the user leaves anisotropic filtering at a low value (the historic default was 4x), oblique ground is sampled from coarser mips**, which again reduces the screen area where level 0 is used. Whether 4x is still the X-Plane 12 default was not established. OWN INFERENCE.
- The RCAS announcement in a release described as "primarily a graphics release", after the 2022 complaints about FSR blur, is compatible with RCAS having been added to counter softness. Laminar does not say so anywhere I could read. OWN INFERENCE, weak.

## 2. TAA literature at source; MSAA and FXAA and texture interiors

### Takeaway
The primary sources confirm the first-round "unverified recollection": TAA blurs through repeated history resampling and through history clamping of small sharp features; a negative mip bias is standard because TAA would otherwise over-blur mip-filtered textures; high-contrast sub-pixel detail makes clamping fail and produces flicker; and a post-TAA sharpening filter is common practice. MSAA shades once per pixel and "does nothing for shading aliasing"; FXAA is a luma-contrast edge filter that blurs high-contrast texture detail.

### Cited Findings

**Karis 2014**
- TALK `[OPENED]` — Brian Karis (Epic Games), "High Quality Temporal Supersampling", SIGGRAPH 2014 course "Advances in Real-Time Rendering in Games" (slides and speaker notes). Slide "MSAA?": "Don't want to shade more than once per pixel / Doesn't affect shading aliasing / More significant aliasing inside triangles than at their edges". Speaker note: "Even if the cost weren't a problem, MSAA only reduces geometric aliasing. It does nothing for shading aliasing." — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, on spatial filters (MLAA, FXAA, SMAA), speaker note: "These are essentially edge finding algorithms with the goal of reducing stair stepping. Our problem is primarily not stepping. It is subpixel aliasing which a spatial filter can't possibly reconstruct. Even on simple stair stepping it should be noted that spatial filters won't be temporally stable." — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, slide "Flickering": "Camera is static but some pixels flicker / Missing subpixel feature's history gets clamped / ... Clamping is an instantaneous impulse / This leads to saw tooth waves which appear as flickering". Note: "When a feature is small enough to not be present in any of the 9 pixels in the neighborhood the history of it will be clamped out as if it disappeared." Anti-flicker remedy "Reduce blend factor when history is near clamping", with the verdict "Not completely solved / Extremely difficult! / Impossible to solve multiple opposing clamps". — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, slide "Blurring: filter kernel": "Mipmap bias all textures / Incorrect derivatives for supersampling / If low contrast then reduce filter kernel size / Technically aliases but looks fine / Can add additional post sharpen filter". Slide "Blurring: reprojection diffusion" names history resampling as the second blur source. — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, slide on post-filter order, speaker note: "Any spatial filter applied before temporal AA which expands an aliased signal larger than 3x3 pixels will flicker. Neighborhood clamping sees this as a change." And: "Neighborhood clamping only works with reasonable levels of noise." — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)

**Yang, Liu & Salvi 2020**
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — Lei Yang, Shiqiu Liu, Marco Salvi (NVIDIA), "A Survey of Temporal Antialiasing Techniques", Computer Graphics Forum 39(2), Eurographics 2020 State of the Art Report (read from the first author's PDF). On MSAA: it works "by limiting the rate of shading to a single per-primitive invocation for each pixel, effectively decoupling visibility determination from shading." — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — same, section on jitter and texture filtering: "With TAA, sampling and integrating over pixel area provides antialiasing for both geometry and texture. Since textures are usually filtered using mipmapping, they may risk being overblurred by TAA in the output. Therefore, typically a mipmap bias is applied to the forward pass where textures are sampled. This is particularly important for temporal upsampling ... The level of mipmap bias should compensate for the ratio between the effective sample density and the input pixel density. For example, if the effective sample count per-input-pixel is expected to be 4 ... then the mipmap bias is calculated as −½ log2 4 = −1.0. In practice, a less aggressive bias between this value and 0 is sometimes preferred to avoid hurting temporal stability as well as texture cache efficiency." — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — same, §6.1 "Blurriness": "There are two sources that accounts for the 'soft' look of TAA results: the error introduced by resampling when history is reprojected, and the error introduced by history rectification where accumulated detail in history color is clipped or clamped." §6.1.1: "bilinear filters soften the resampled image ... Since the history is reprojected in every frame, the filtering error accumulates and quickly result in an objectionable, blurry look." — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — same, §6.1.2: history rectification assumes "that the current frame samples in the neighborhood of each pixel contain the entire gamut of surface colors covered by that pixel ... Unfortunately, with highly detailed content, this assumption is often violated ... This happens commonly in highly detailed scenes, where small, sharp features are smoothed out in the output". Then: "To regain some of the sharp, detailed appearance, some renderers apply a sharpening filter, such as a discrete Laplacian, to the result of TAA output [Dro14, Xu16, Wih17]. An adaptive sharpening filter [Kra19] can be used to avoid producing out-of-gamut colors in sharpening." — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — same, §6.3 "Temporal instability": "history rejection or rectification algorithms may mistakenly invalidate or clamp history due to jitter-induced sample color change. Figure 11 shows such an example where a low frequency Moiré pattern causes a complete gamut change in the neighborhood of some pixels, forcing the history color to be clipped or clamped to the new sample value in every frame, effectively disabling accumulation. Disabling or relaxing history rectification avoids the flickering ... but may expose the output to ghosting or temporal lag. In fact, avoiding ghosting and flickering are often contradicting goals". — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — same, introduction: post-processing AA techniques proposed to replace MSAA in deferred rendering "suffer from temporal stability problems due to a lack of sufficient information to recover true pixel values." — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)

**MSAA and FXAA**
- ENCYCLOPEDIA `[OPENED]` — Wikipedia, "Multisample anti-aliasing" (read 27 November 2026), quoting the OpenGL `GL_ARB_multisample` specification: "The specification dictates that the renderer evaluate the fragment program once per pixel, and only 'truly' supersample the depth and stencil values." And: "The pixel shader usually only needs to be evaluated once per pixel for every triangle covering at least one sample point. The edges of polygons (the most obvious source of aliasing in 3D graphics) are anti-aliased." The article lists alpha testing and (shader/texture) aliasing under disadvantages. — [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multisample_anti-aliasing)
- ENCYCLOPEDIA `[OPENED]` — Wikipedia, "Fast approximate anti-aliasing" (read 27 November 2026): FXAA is "a screen-space anti-aliasing algorithm created by Timothy Lottes at Nvidia"; it finds "high contrast pixels by using a high pass filter that uses the luminance data"; "Since it is not based on the actual geometry, it will smooth not only edges between triangles, but also edges inside alpha-blended textures, or those resulting from pixel shader effects, which are immune to the effects of multisample anti-aliasing (MSAA). The downsides are that high contrast texture maps are blurred, effect that can be reduced by adjusting the 'subpix filtering'". — [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_approximate_anti-aliasing)
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, "FXAA: Your New Friend", 23 September 2011: "Anisotropic filtering will not fix this because the aliasing is coming from the per-pixel alpha cutout, not from reading the texture itself ... With FXAA the aliasing is less ugly (as FXAA smooths the errors) but it cannot reconstruct the lost resolution." — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2011/09/fxaa-your-new-friend/)
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, "A Flicker of Hope for Flicker", 1 November 2012: users reporting "flicker" were reporting temporal aliasing of long thin screen elements such as roofs and roads: "As the camera moves, which pixels form the stair-step will change every frame, causing parts of the road or roof to flicker into and out of existence on a per frame basis." "SSAA does better at fixing temporal artifacts like flicker (because it makes things '4x bigger' relative to the size at which they flicker) but FXAA does better at making diagonals 'less jagged'." — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2012/11/a-flicker-of-hope-for-flicker/)

### Inferences
- The first-round inference that MSAA leaves texture interiors untouched is now sourced (Karis; Yang et al.; OpenGL specification via Wikipedia). In X-Plane 12 with MSAA, the orthophoto interior is band-limited only by mip and anisotropic filtering. OWN INFERENCE from the sources.
- The first-round inference about FXAA is sourced in direction: FXAA blurs high-contrast texture detail. A stronger unsharp mask therefore puts more orthophoto pixels above FXAA's contrast threshold, and FXAA partially undoes the sharpening there, while (Karis) remaining temporally unstable. No source measured this for sharpened textures. OWN INFERENCE.
- Under TAA, "small, sharp features" and "high local contrast" are the documented failure cases for clamping. Offline sharpening moves texture content toward that failure case. For X-Plane this stays hypothetical while there is no TAA.

### Gaps
- The FXAA white paper (Lottes, NVIDIA) was not opened: `developer.download.nvidia.com` is not reachable. FXAA is therefore sourced only through Wikipedia, Karis's notes and Laminar's 2011 post.
- Pedersen 2016, Salvi 2016, Jimenez 2016/2017 were not opened; they are cited here only as they appear inside the survey.
- No source measures flicker or blur as a function of texture pre-sharpening strength.

## 3. FSR 1, CAS and RCAS at source (gpuopen.com and the SIGGRAPH 2021 talk)

### Takeaway
At source: FSR 1 is purely spatial, "is not an anti-aliasing solution by itself" and "does definitely require good AA going into it"; noise and other high-frequency components should be added after upscaling "to avoid those noisy components being amplified"; the mip bias recommended for FSR 1 is log2(render resolution / display resolution) with no extra −1. The gpuopen.com FSR 1 page itself does not state the mip bias; that comes from the talk and from the SDK documentation read in round 1.

### Cited Findings
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — AMD GPUOpen, "AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution 1 (FSR 1)" product page (read 27 November 2026; no page date): "AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution is a spatial upscaler: it works by taking the current anti-aliased frame and upscaling it to display resolution without relying on other data such as frame history or motion vectors." "FSR provides consistent upscaling quality regardless of whether the frame is in movement". RCAS is "A sharpening pass called RCAS (Robust Contrast-Adaptive Sharpening) that extracts pixel detail in the upscaled image." — [gpuopen.com](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — same page: "AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution passes work best in perceptual color space, and should therefore be integrated after tone mapping. Passes that introduce noise or other high-frequency visual components to the scene should be rendered after upscaling to avoid those noisy components being amplified." — [gpuopen.com](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — same page, quality modes: Ultra Quality 1.3x per dimension, Quality 1.5x, Balanced 1.7x, Performance 2.0x. (Confirms the scale factors that were "from memory" in round 1.) — [gpuopen.com](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — Timothy Lottes (Unity, formerly AMD; author of FSR 1.0) and Kleber Garcia (Unity), "FidelityFX Super Resolution 1.0" (slides with speaker notes, SIGGRAPH 2021 Advances in Real-Time Rendering in Games). Lottes: the design goal was "something which adds no temporal artifacts ... So if you use an anti-aliasing which adds no temporal artifacts and you process through this algorithm, it cannot be adding any temporal artifacts which didn't exist. Thus for FSR 1.0 the algorithm focused on spatial scaling and spatial sharpening." — [SIGGRAPH 2021 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Unity%20AMD%20FSR%20-%20SIGGRAPH%202021.pdf)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, Lottes: "Due to this adaptability EASU requires input with good anti-aliasing as a base. It is not an anti-aliasing solution by itself, and thus it does definitely require good AA going into it." — [SIGGRAPH 2021 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Unity%20AMD%20FSR%20-%20SIGGRAPH%202021.pdf)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — same, Garcia on Unity's integration: "The next step is to take care of the extra detail that we lose because of the derivatives that we are doing when we are rendering at a lower rasterization resolution. What we are doing in this case is pushing the mip bias in all our material textures so that we can recover this detail ... The mip bias formula that we use is the ratio of the input resolution divided by the output resolution. Then what we do is that we take the log base 2 of this ratio and we get a negative mip bias". On RCAS: "The RCAS algorithm improves on edging and all the details that were potentially loss before." — [SIGGRAPH 2021 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Unity%20AMD%20FSR%20-%20SIGGRAPH%202021.pdf)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — AMD GPUOpen, "AMD FidelityFX Contrast Adaptive Sharpening (CAS)" product page (read 27 November 2026), headline "Adaptive sharpening for TAA": "The algorithm adjusts the amount of sharpening per pixel to target an even level of sharpness across the image. Areas of the input image that are already sharp are sharpened less, while areas that lack detail are sharpened more. This allows for higher overall natural visual sharpness with fewer artifacts." And: "CAS was designed to help increase the quality of existing Temporal Anti-Aliasing (TAA) solutions. TAA often introduces a variable amount of blur due to temporal feedback. The adaptive sharpening provided by CAS is ideal to restore detail in images produced after TAA." — [gpuopen.com](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-cas/)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_fsr1.h`, RCAS code re-read this round: `hitMin = min(mn4,e) / (4*mx4)`, `hitMax = (1 - max(mx4,e)) / (4*mn4 - 4)`, `lobe = max(-hitMin, hitMax)` per channel, then `lobe = max(-FSR_RCAS_LIMIT, min(max3(lobeR,lobeG,lobeB), 0)) * sharpness`, with `FSR_RCAS_LIMIT (0.25-(1.0/16.0))`; `mn4`/`mx4` are the minimum and maximum of the four cross neighbours. — [ffx_fsr1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — Lottes (AMD), `ffx_cas.h`, CAS code re-read this round: `amp = saturate(min(mn, 1.0 - mx) / mx)`, then `amp = sqrt(amp)`, then `w = amp * peak`, where `mn`/`mx` are the minimum and maximum of the 3x3 (cross plus diagonals) neighbourhood. — [ffx_cas.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/master/ffx-cas/ffx_cas.h)

### Inferences
- FSR 1's recommended bias exactly cancels the mip shift caused by the lower render resolution (textures are sampled as if at display resolution). Relative to the render-resolution pixel grid this is undersampling by the scale factor: at Performance mode (2.0x) a bias of −1 means roughly two texels per rendered pixel. OWN INFERENCE from the formula.

- The gpuopen.com FSR 1 page contains no mip-bias text and no statement of RCAS sharpness units; those remain sourced from `ffx_fsr1.h` and the SDK docs (round 1) and from the talk (this round).
- AMD's default RCAS sharpness in its sample was not found.

## 4. Counter-evidence to the first-round conclusions

### Takeaway
Two first-round conclusions need correcting. First, CAS and RCAS do reduce their strength as local contrast rises, not only near black and white: sharpening is at full strength only where the local min/max ratio is above about 0.75, so strong unsharp-mask halos are sharpened again only weakly, while low-contrast fine detail and noise get the full second boost. Second, the vendors' negative-bias recommendations exist because temporal accumulation (TAA, FSR 2, DLSS) integrates the extra detail; X-Plane has no such accumulation, so that justification does not carry over, and FSR 1's own bias only restores display-resolution sampling.

### Cited Findings

**4a. "Pre-sharpened texture + RCAS/CAS = halos sharpened again at near full strength"**
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — AMD's product page states the opposite design intent: "Areas of the input image that are already sharp are sharpened less, while areas that lack detail are sharpened more." — [gpuopen.com](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-cas/)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — The code supports AMD's statement. In CAS, `amp = sqrt(min(mn, 1−mx)/mx)`: in the darker half of the range this equals `sqrt(mn/mx)`, a pure function of the local min/max ratio. — [ffx_cas.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-CAS/master/ffx-cas/ffx_cas.h)
- VENDOR DOCUMENTATION `[OPENED]` — In RCAS the lobe magnitude is `min(0.1875, mn/(4·mx), (1−mx)/(4·(1−mn)))` times the sharpness factor (rearranged from the code above). — [ffx_fsr1.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GPUOpen-Effects/FidelityFX-FSR/master/ffx-fsr/ffx_fsr1.h)
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — Yang et al. describe adaptive sharpening as a way "to avoid producing out-of-gamut colors in sharpening", i.e. the adaptivity is an anti-clipping device. — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)

**4b. "Without TAA, ground-texture shimmer is governed only by mip/anisotropic filtering"**
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, 4 March 2022: "the engine has a history buffer". And 14 September 2022, on magenta NaN artefacts: values "from the past frame – they get 'reflected' by SSR and propagate from one part of the frame to the other." So X-Plane 12 does reuse the previous frame, for screen-space reflections. — [comment 41061](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/03/x-plane-12-development-update-march-4th-2022/#comment-41061), [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — Ben Supnik, 14 September 2022: FSR below 100 % renders "at a lower resolution"; "a less detailed image on purpose just like reducing resolution does". — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — Lottes 2021: FSR 1 "cannot be adding any temporal artifacts which didn't exist" (and, being spatial, removes none). — [SIGGRAPH 2021 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Unity%20AMD%20FSR%20-%20SIGGRAPH%202021.pdf)

**4c. "Negative mip bias causes shimmer per vendor docs" versus "vendors recommend negative bias"**
- PEER-REVIEWED `[OPENED]` — Yang et al.: the bias exists because textures "may risk being overblurred by TAA", and it "should compensate for the ratio between the effective sample density and the input pixel density"; "a less aggressive bias ... is sometimes preferred to avoid hurting temporal stability". The recommendation is conditional on accumulated samples. — [TemporalAA.pdf](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/behindthepixels/behindthepixels.github.io/master/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — Karis 2014: "Mipmap bias all textures ... Technically aliases but looks fine" in the context of temporal supersampling. — [TemporalAA.pptx](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2014/epic/TemporalAA.pptx)
- TALK `[OPENED]` — Garcia 2021 (FSR 1 in Unity): bias = log2(input resolution / output resolution), no additional term. Compare round 1: FSR 2/3 and DLSS add −1.0 on top. — [SIGGRAPH 2021 PDF](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2021/Unity%20AMD%20FSR%20-%20SIGGRAPH%202021.pdf)

**4d. Laminar's reasons for sharpening or not sharpening**
- LAMINAR STATEMENT `[OPENED]` — The only statement is the one-line 12.1.0 roadmap mention of RCAS (9 February 2024). No rationale is given on the developer blog. — [developer.x-plane.com](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/02/blog-post-walk-of-shame/)

### Inferences
- **CORRECTION to round 1, section 4.** Round 1 said the CAS/RCAS adaptivity is "about headroom to 0/1, not about 'already sharp'" and that a mid-tone halo "is amplified a second time" at close to full strength. Evaluating the code read this round gives a different picture (OWN INFERENCE, arithmetic on the documented formulas, values in the 0..1 space the filter runs in):

  | Local neighbourhood (min – max) | CAS `amp` (1 = full) | RCAS lobe as share of its maximum 0.1875 |
  |---|---|---|
  | 0.48 – 0.52 (flat, fine texture) | 0.96 | 100 % |
  | 0.30 – 0.60 | 0.71 | 67 % |
  | 0.20 – 0.80 | 0.50 | 33 % |
  | 0.10 – 0.90 (strong edge / halo) | 0.33 | 15 % |

  RCAS runs at full strength only when min/max ≥ 0.75 and (1−max)/(1−min) ≥ 0.75 in the 5-tap cross. Both filters therefore do sharpen high-local-contrast neighbourhoods less, including in the mid-tones. The mechanism is still a clipping bound, as round 1 said, but local contrast consumes that bound, so in effect it is contrast-adaptive, as AMD states.
- **What this means for stacking.** An unsharp-mask halo at a strong edge (roof edge, road marking) is a high-contrast neighbourhood, so RCAS adds comparatively little there. The second sharpening lands in full on low-contrast fine detail: field texture, sensor noise, JPEG and BC1 block structure, which the offline unsharp mask has already amplified. The first-round conclusion "double sharpening" survives for noise and fine grain and is weakened for edge halos. Because pre-sharpening raises local contrast everywhere, it also moves more of the image out of RCAS's full-strength regime, so the two stages are partly self-limiting. OWN INFERENCE.
- **Residual risk the adaptivity does not remove.** The clipping bound is computed after the texture has been filtered, lit, tone-mapped and (with FSR) upscaled. Overshoot and clipping baked into the texture offline are not undone. And RCAS's adaptivity only limits its own contribution; it does not reduce the texture's contrast. OWN INFERENCE.
- **4b, refined.** The round-1 statement stands for the ground albedo in substance, with two qualifications: (1) X-Plane does keep a previous-frame buffer and uses it for screen-space reflections, so it is not correct to say the pipeline has no temporal reuse at all; nothing read suggests this buffer filters the terrain albedo. (2) With FSR below 100 % and no compensating mip bias, coarser mips are selected (+0.38 LOD at 1.3x, +0.58 at 1.5x, +0.77 at 1.7x, +1.0 at 2.0x, by log2 of the confirmed scale factors), which reduces texture shimmer and at the same time hides a level-0-only sharpening over a larger part of the screen. Whether X-Plane applies the bias is unknown, so which of the two regimes applies is unknown. MSAA sample-rate shading and alpha-to-coverage: no Laminar statement found; alpha-to-coverage would in any case affect alpha-tested geometry, not opaque terrain. OWN INFERENCE.
- **4c, clarified.** The two vendor positions are consistent once the reason is read at source: a negative bias is recommended where several jittered samples per output pixel are accumulated over time, so that sampling "sharper than the render pixel" is integrated back into a correctly filtered pixel; and even there the survey and the vendor guides advise backing off for high-frequency content. X-Plane has no jitter and no accumulation. Any texture energy above what one sample per pixel can represent reaches the screen as frame-to-frame variation when the camera moves. A sharpened level 0 increases exactly that energy at the distances where level 0 maps roughly 1:1 to pixels. So the vendors' acceptance of negative bias is not evidence that X-Plane can absorb extra texture sharpness; it is evidence that such sharpness needs a temporal integrator that X-Plane does not have. OWN INFERENCE from Yang et al., Karis, and the Laminar statements.
- **FSR 1's own bias is a different case.** It restores display-resolution texture sampling and asks for nothing sharper than native rendering would use. Under FSR 1 with that bias, the rendered frame is undersampled relative to its own pixel grid by the scale factor, with no temporal compensation; AMD's requirement of "good AA going into it" is not met for texture interiors by MSAA. A pre-sharpened texture makes this input less compliant, not more. OWN INFERENCE.
- **Counter-point in favour of sharpening.** Laminar's default pipeline at native resolution (FSR at 100 %, RCAS off or low, no TAA) contains no blur stage of the kind that CAS was "designed to help" with. So there is no TAA softness for an offline sharpening to fight against, but also no accumulated blur that would hide a modest sharpening. The case for mild pre-sharpening is therefore not refuted by these sources; what they refute is the idea that the display pipeline will tame an aggressive one. OWN INFERENCE.

### Gaps
- No measurement exists, in anything read, of pre-sharpened textures passed through RCAS or CAS. The table above is arithmetic on the shader formulas, not a measured result; real neighbourhoods are per-channel and RCAS takes the weakest channel lobe.
- Whether X-Plane's RCAS runs in perceptual or linear space, at which strength, and whether it is active at native resolution: unknown (release notes not readable).
- Whether X-Plane compensates mip bias under FSR: unknown.
- No Laminar statement on why RCAS was added, and none on sharpening of textures or orthophotos.
- A direct A/B test in X-Plane 12.4.x (sharpened versus unsharpened tile; FSR 100 % versus Quality; RCAS on/off; MSAA versus FXAA; low-altitude motion; Texture Browser "Pager Info" checked for target scale ≥ 1) remains the only way to settle the practical question.
