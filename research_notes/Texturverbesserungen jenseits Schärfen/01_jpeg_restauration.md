# Decoder-side JPEG restoration before texture encoding (beyond sharpening)

Scope: candidates for improving decoded 256 px JPEG chunks (quality ~75-85, mostly 4:2:0) before mip
building and BC1. Budget reference: about 80 ms assembly + 42 ms BC1 per 4096x4096 tile on one core;
an added step should cost a few ms to low tens of ms per tile (16.8 MP).

Source rule used here: every item under "Cited Findings" comes from a document that was downloaded
and read in this session, marked [OPENED]. Labels: MEASURED (numbers measured by the source's
authors), AUTHOR CLAIM, VENDOR CLAIM, BLOG OPINION, OWN INFERENCE (mine, derived from opened
sources; arithmetic shown). Items known only from search snippets or that could not be fetched are
in "Unverified (not opened)" lists inside each Gaps section and must not be cited as facts.

## 1. Quantisation-constrained / DCT-domain restoration (jpeg2png, Knusperli, Quant Smooth, block smoothing, POCS, re-application of JPEG, SA-DCT, BM3D)

### Takeaway
At quality ~80 the only opened measurement that covers this whole family (Shu & Wu 2016, QF = 80) shows the cheap members doing nothing or harm: a block-boundary deblocker gains +0.00 dB, TV-regularised quantisation-constrained decoding (the jpeg2png family) loses 0.80 dB, while only heavy methods (oracle BM3D, sparse/low-rank soft decoding) gain 1-2 dB at costs of seconds to minutes per megapixel. None of the opened implementations (jpeg2png, Knusperli, Quant Smooth) publishes a PSNR/SSIM number at q = 75-85; the one Rust decoder that ships Knusperli states it "hurts at Q70+".

### Cited Findings

**jpeg2png (TV/TGV smoothing under quantisation constraints)**
- What it does: finds "the smoothest possible picture that encodes to the given JPEG file": minimises total (generalised) variation plus a small DCT-deviation term, projecting each DCT coefficient back into its quantisation interval `[x-0.5, x+0.5]`; projected subgradient method with FISTA acceleration. AUTHOR CLAIM/description — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- Evidence at q 75-85: none. The README shows two examples (Lena at 10 % quality 4:2:0; a drawing at 90 % 4:4:4) as images only, no PSNR/SSIM figure; its wishlist still lists "make comparisons with known JPEG artifact reduction techniques" as open. — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- Suitability statement by the author: "jpeg2png gives best results for pictures that should never be saved as JPEG. Examples are charts, logo's, and cartoon-style digital drawings. On the other hand, jpeg2png gives poor result for photographs or other finely textured pictures." AUTHOR CLAIM — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- Cost: "A low number of steps, like 10, will take only a few seconds ... A high number of steps, like 1000, might take a few minutes" (image size not stated); SSE2 versions give 2x over plain C; the wishlist mentions "improving time from 40 to 18 seconds". AUTHOR CLAIM — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- Implementation: C11, depends on libjpeg and libpng, licence GPLv3+. — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- Independent measurement of the same method family at QF 80: the "TV" comparison method in Shu & Wu is Bredies & Holler's "total variation-based JPEG decompression model" (which jpeg2png's README cites as its reference [4] in the TGV variant). On ten 256x256 test images at QF = 80 it changes PSNR by a median of **-0.80 dB** (range -2.59 to +1.01) and SSIM by a median of **-0.0043**; at QF = 50 -0.08 dB; at QF = 25 +0.11 dB. MEASURED — [Shu-Wu 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01339), Tables 1-3 and 6

**Knusperli (Google)**
- What it does: interprets "quantized DCT coefficients in the image data as an interval, rather than a fixed value", and chooses "the value from that interval that minimizes discontinuities at block boundaries". The demonstration image in the README is baboon at q50. No numbers, no timing. Licence Apache-2.0, C++, Bazel build; "not an officially supported Google product". — [knusperli](https://github.com/google/knusperli)
- Competing author's assessment: "you can hardly see any improvements on the image", "no performance optimizations (but roughly same speed as for quantsmooth with optimizations)", "knusperli is good for nothing, in my opinion". BLOG OPINION (author of a competing tool) — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- A Rust decoder that ships a Knusperli mode documents it as: quality gain "+14 at Q5-Q10, **hurts at Q70+**" (metric: the vendor's own "zensim vs original"), "20-40% slower" decode, forces a buffered coefficient path. Its simple alternative `Boundary4Tap` is listed at "+0.5 at Q90, +2 at Q50, +10 at Q10" for "+2% scanline" cost. VENDOR CLAIM (test set and metric scale not described in the README) — [zenjpeg](https://github.com/imazen/zenjpeg)

**JPEG Quant Smooth (jpeg-quantsmooth)**
- What it does: "tries to recover the lost precision of DCT coefficients based on a quantization table from a JPEG image"; output coefficients can be kept inside the original quantisation intervals ("will result in the same DCT coefficients as in the original image"). Default 3 iterations; "More iterations can make the result look like CG art, can make the photos look unnatural." AUTHOR CLAIM — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- Evidence: example strips of an image "with quality increasing from 8% to 98%" as pictures only; no PSNR/SSIM numbers at any quality. The motivation given is print and re-editing: "You may not notice jpeg artifacts on the screen without zooming in". AUTHOR CLAIM — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- Cost: no absolute timings in the README. Relative statements only: quality level 4 is "~1.5 times slower" than the default, levels 0-2 (`LOW_QUALITY`) are "~10 times faster, but the quality is lower", and jpeg2png is "10 to 20 times slower" than Quant Smooth. Has scalar/SSE2/AVX2/AVX512 paths and OpenMP threads. AUTHOR CLAIM — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- Implementation: C, built on libjpeg/libjpeg-turbo (replaces `jpeg_start_decompress`/`jpeg_finish_decompress` with `jpegqs_` versions, usable as a library `libjpegqs.a`), also WebAssembly. LICENSE file is LGPL-2.1. — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- Quality level 6 adds `UPSAMPLE_UV`: "non-blurring chroma upsampling, unlike fancy upsampling from libjpeg"; level 5 adds `JOINT_YUV`: "chroma channels will depend from luminance, better color consistency". AUTHOR CLAIM, no numbers — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)

**libjpeg / libjpeg-turbo / mozjpeg "block smoothing"**
- `do_block_smoothing`: "If TRUE, interblock smoothing is applied in early stages of decoding progressive JPEG files; if FALSE, not. Default is TRUE. ... block smoothing ceases to be applied after the first few AC coefficients are known to full accuracy, so it is relevant only when using buffered-image mode for progressive images." A second passage: "This setting is relevant only when decoding a progressive JPEG image." — [libjpeg.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libjpeg-turbo/libjpeg-turbo/main/doc/libjpeg.txt)
- The Rust wrapper exposes the same switch (`Decompress::do_block_smoothing`) with the same doc comment. — [mozjpeg-rust src](https://github.com/ImageOptim/mozjpeg-rust)

**BM3D, low-rank and sparse "soft decoding" (the heavy end)**
- Shu & Wu, ten 256x256 images, QF = 80, median PSNR gain over standard decoding / median SSIM gain (baseline JPEG median 36.93 dB, SSIM 0.9627): ACR deblocking (Zhai et al. 2008, coefficient regularisation + shape-adaptive filtering + quantisation constraint) **+0.00 dB / +0.0001**; BM3D given the *true* error variance +0.94 dB / +0.0060; WNNM +1.21 dB / +0.0048; TV (Bredies-Holler) -0.80 dB / -0.0043; DicTV (learned dictionary + TV) **-1.43 dB / -0.0180**; DTPD (Liu et al., CVPR 2015) +1.69 dB / +0.0110; proposed low-rank method +2.12 dB / +0.0126. MEASURED — [Shu-Wu 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01339), Tables 3 and 6
- Same paper at QF = 50 / 25 for comparison: ACR +0.05 / +0.22 dB, BM3D +1.04 / +1.01 dB, TV -0.08 / +0.11 dB, proposed +2.06 / +1.81 dB. So block-boundary deblocking and TV decoding lose their benefit as quality rises, while BM3D-class methods keep about 1 dB. MEASURED — [Shu-Wu 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01339), Tables 1-2
- Cost: the proposed method's reference implementation is "pure MATLAB", slower than BM3D; no absolute seconds are given in the paper. — [Shu-Wu 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01339)
- BM3D run time on CPU (Gaussian denoising, as measured by the DnCNN authors): 0.65 s for 256x256, 2.85 s for 512x512, 11.89 s for 1024x1024. MEASURED — [DnCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981), Table IV
- Pointwise SA-DCT (Foi et al.): "it needs 3.4 seconds to process a 256 x 256 image" in C++ on an Intel i3 3.1 GHz, as measured by the ARCNN authors. MEASURED (by a competitor) — [ARCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06993)

**General**
- "No post-processing technique has been shown to improve image quality in all cases; consequently, none has garnered widespread acceptance, though some have been implemented and are in use in proprietary systems." (Wikipedia editorial statement, cites a PC Magazine glossary entry) — [Wiki CA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_artifact)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: "simple deblocking is neutral at q 79-85" in the project's own bench (<= +0.0003 SSIM) agrees with the only opened external number for a quantisation-aware deblocker at QF 80 (ACR: +0.00 dB, +0.0001 SSIM, Shu-Wu Table 3/6) and with zenjpeg's "+0.5 at Q90" for its boundary filter. Two independent sources plus the project's bench point the same way: boundary-only deblocking has no demonstrated gain at this quality.
- OWN INFERENCE: jpeg2png-style TV/TGV decoding should be expected to *lose* fidelity on q~80 aerial imagery. Reasons: (a) measured -0.80 dB / -0.0043 SSIM at QF 80 for the TV decompression model; (b) the author's own statement that it "gives poor result for photographs or other finely textured pictures", and orthophotos at 2.4 m/pixel are finely textured; (c) the project's bench found the providers' zoom 16 already carries 0.95-0.97 of the truth's gradient energy, so any smoothing prior removes real detail.
- OWN INFERENCE (cost, by pixel-count scaling to 4096x4096 = 16.8 MP = 256 x a 256x256 image, single core): SA-DCT 3.4 s x 256 = about 870 s; BM3D 11.89 s x 16 = about 190 s. Both are 3-4 orders of magnitude over a 10 ms budget. jpeg2png ("a few seconds" at 10 steps for an unstated image size) is likewise out. Quant Smooth has no absolute number; given the statement that jpeg2png is 10-20x slower, its default mode is plausibly hundreds of ms to seconds per 16.8 MP, i.e. still outside the budget, but this is not measured.
- OWN INFERENCE: libjpeg's block smoothing cannot help XEL: it only acts on progressive files in buffered-image mode while AC coefficients are still missing, and it is a libjpeg feature, not a zune-jpeg one.
- OWN INFERENCE: the 1-2 dB gains of BM3D/low-rank methods at QF 80 are measured against the *uncompressed original of the same image*. XEL's bench measures against a zoom-18 reference reduced to zoom 16, where JPEG error is only one part of the difference (different resampling, possibly different captures on ArcGIS). A 1 dB reduction of JPEG error would show up as a much smaller gain in that metric. Whether it is visible after BC1 (itself a lossy 4x4 block codec) is untested.

### Gaps
- No opened source gives PSNR/SSIM for jpeg2png, Knusperli or Quant Smooth at q 75-85, and none gives absolute runtime per megapixel for Knusperli or Quant Smooth.
- POCS (Zakhor 1992; Yang, Galatsanos, Katsaggelos 1993/1995): no primary paper could be opened (IEEE-hosted). No numbers reported here.
- Nosratinia, "Enhancement of JPEG-Compressed Images by Re-application of JPEG" (J. VLSI Signal Processing, 2001): not opened (publisher-hosted). No numbers reported here.
- Foi, Katkovnik, Egiazarian, "Pointwise Shape-Adaptive DCT for High-Quality Denoising and Deblocking of Grayscale and Color Images", IEEE TIP 16(5), 2007: the authors' results page (webpages.tuni.fi/foi/SA-DCT/results.html) could not be fetched from this environment; only the runtime quoted by ARCNN is verified. Its gain at q 75-85 is unknown to me.
- The Shu-Wu comparison uses ten 256x256 classic test images (Lenna, Barbara, ...), MATLAB JPEG, grayscale presumably; none is aerial imagery. Transfer to orthophotos is not shown.
- Unverified (not opened, search snippets only): an EUSIPCO 2006 SA-DCT paper reportedly showing Lena at Q=6 from 28.24 to 29.87 dB; the claim that shifted-transform methods' "computational effort ... has prevented their widespread use".

## 2. Learned restoration (ARCNN, DnCNN, QGAC, FBCNN, SwinIR, RBQE)

### Takeaway
Learned models are the only family with a demonstrated, sizeable gain at quality 80 against the uncompressed original (QGAC: roughly +1.3 to +1.6 dB PSNR at quality 80, dropping steeply at 90), but almost all published tables stop at QF 40-50, and every published runtime is two to four orders of magnitude over XEL's per-tile budget on CPU and still seconds per tile on a GPU that X-Plane is also using.

### Cited Findings
- **Quality range actually tested.** ARCNN: q = 10, 20 (plus 30, 40 models), LIVE1/BSDS500 — [ARCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06993). DnCNN-3: trained on QF 5-99, evaluated on "quality factors 10, 20, 30 and 40" — [DnCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981). SwinIR: "JPEG quality factors 10, 20, 30 and 40" — [SwinIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10257). FBCNN: trained with QF "randomly sampled from 10 to 95", single-JPEG tables at QF 10-40 — [FBCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14573). RBQE: JPEG QF up to 50 — [RBQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16581). QGAC: trained on quality 10-100 in steps of 10; tables at 10/20/30, plus one figure covering 10-100 — [QGAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09320).
- **The one opened result at quality 80.** QGAC Fig. 11 ("Increase in PSNR on color datasets ... average improvement in PSNR values on qualities 10-100. Improvement drops off steeply at quality 90."). Values read off the bar chart by me (approximate, +-0.1 dB): quality 80: Live-1 about +1.6 dB, BSDS500 about +1.5 dB, ICB about +1.25 dB; quality 90: Live-1 about +0.7 dB, ICB about +0.4 dB, BSDS500 about 0; quality 100: about 0. For comparison quality 10: about +2.0 / +1.9 / +2.75 dB. MEASURED by the authors, read from a figure — [QGAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09320)
- QGAC says about high quality: "the filter response becomes smaller as the quality increases since the filters have to do less 'work' to correct a high quality JPEG." AUTHOR CLAIM — [QGAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09320)
- **Low-quality reference numbers** (colour, LIVE1, PSNR | SSIM): QF 40: JPEG 30.28 | 0.882, QGAC 32.05 | 0.912, FBCNN 32.34 | 0.913; QF 10: JPEG 25.69 | 0.743, FBCNN 27.77 | 0.803. MEASURED — [FBCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14573), Table 2
- **Failure mode relevant to tile pyramids.** "existing methods are prone to fail on non-aligned double JPEG images even with only a one-pixel shift"; this happens when the 8x8 grids of two compressions are not aligned and QF1 <= QF2. FBCNN adds a double-JPEG degradation model for this. AUTHOR CLAIM with experiments — [FBCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14573) and [FBCNN README](https://github.com/jiaxi-jiang/FBCNN)
- **Controllability.** FBCNN predicts a quality factor and lets the user override it "to control the trade-off between artifacts removal and details preservation". AUTHOR CLAIM — [FBCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.14573)
- **Runtime.**
  - ARCNN: 0.5 s for a 256x256 image, C++ on Intel i3 3.1 GHz CPU. MEASURED — [ARCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.06993)
  - DnCNN (CPU / GPU seconds, GPU = cuDNN v5, transfer time not counted): DnCNN-S 0.74 / 0.014 at 256x256, 3.41 / 0.051 at 512x512, 12.1 / 0.200 at 1024x1024; DnCNN-B 0.90 / 0.016, 4.11 / 0.060, 14.1 / 0.235. MEASURED — [DnCNN](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.03981), Table IV
  - QGAC: measured in FPS "on our NVIDIA Pascal GPU for 100 720p (1280 x 720) frames"; the plot's FPS axis runs 0 to 6 and all compared networks (IDCN, MWCNN, DMCNN, QGAC) lie inside it, i.e. single-digit frames per second at 0.92 MP. MEASURED (figure; exact QGAC value not legible in text extraction) — [QGAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09320), Appendix B.5 / Fig. 8
  - RBQE: 17.9 to 27.5 GMacs per image for easy to hard samples vs 77.8 (DCAD), 118.4 (QE-CNN), 160.5 (CBDNet), 175.8 (DnCNN) GMacs; early exit: for the easiest samples the first exit is only 0.02 dB below the last one while the last exit costs 270 % of the FLOPs. MEASURED (HEVC-MSP test set; JPEG results "similar", in supplementary) — [RBQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16581)
  - SwinIR JPEG model: 12.0 M parameters (vs DRUNet 32.7 M). — [SwinIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10257)
- **Licences / code.** FBCNN: PyTorch, Apache-2.0 ("This project is released under the Apache 2.0 license") — [FBCNN README](https://github.com/jiaxi-jiang/FBCNN). RBQE code: https://github.com/RyanXingQL/RBQE (URL from the abstract; repo and licence not opened) — [RBQE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16581).

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (cost per 4096x4096 tile, scaling by pixel count): DnCNN-B on CPU 14.1 s x 16 = about 226 s; on a 2016-era GPU 0.235 s x 16 = about 3.8 s. ARCNN on CPU 0.5 s x 256 = about 128 s. QGAC at single-digit FPS for 0.92 MP is 18 x that area, so on the order of 3 to 20 s per tile on that GPU. Newer GPUs are faster, but a 100-1000x gap to a 10 ms budget does not close, and XEL's own documentation says GPU work competes with X-Plane on single-GPU hosts. None of these fits on-demand generation; for background prefetch they would cut prefetch throughput by one to three orders of magnitude.
- OWN INFERENCE: a purpose-built tiny network (a few 3x3 conv layers at 8-16 channels as a wgpu compute pass) could fit a GPU budget in principle, but I opened no source that reports the gain of such a small model at quality 80; the RBQE early-exit result (easy samples lose only 0.02 dB at the first exit) suggests high-quality inputs need little capacity, but that exit is still 17.9 GMacs per image. Gain at q 80 for a real-time-sized model is therefore undemonstrated.
- OWN INFERENCE (hallucination / domain risk): all opened models are trained on DIV2K/Flickr2K-type natural photos and evaluated against the uncompressed original of the same photo. None reports aerial/satellite imagery. Regression-trained (L1/L2) models tend towards smoothing rather than inventing structure; QGAC additionally offers a GAN-trained variant whose purpose is to synthesise texture, which is the wrong choice for imagery that is supposed to represent real ground. The FBCNN double-JPEG finding matters for XEL: provider tiles are resampled pyramids of earlier compressed data, so artefacts from an earlier, non-aligned compression may be present and the "quality" read from the quantisation table would not describe them.
- OWN INFERENCE: the +1.3 to +1.6 dB at quality 80 is against the pre-compression original. XEL's bench target (zoom 18 reduced) would see less, and the project's measurement that a 3x3 sigma deringing filter gains only +0.0002 to +0.0006 SSIM suggests the JPEG-error share of the total difference to truth is small.

### Gaps
- No opened source reports learned JPEG restoration on aerial/satellite orthophotos, nor any gain measured after a BC1 encode.
- No opened source gives CPU or GPU timing for FBCNN or SwinIR-JPEG at 16 MP; SwinIR's runtime sentence was not extractable. FBCNN parameter count not verified.
- "Small real-time variants": I found and opened none with numbers at q >= 75. Unverified.
- QGAC Fig. 11 values are my reading of a bar chart; the paper prints no table for quality 80.

## 3. Chroma handling and decode precision (edge-directed/luma-guided chroma upsampling, jpegli, float IDCT, dithering)

### Takeaway
I found no opened measurement showing that anything beyond triangle chroma upsampling, or a float/high-precision decode, improves 8-bit output at quality ~80. Jpegli's decoder changes (Laplacian-expectation dequantisation, float pipeline, 16-bit/float output) are described by Google without decoder-side numbers; its published evaluation covers the encoder only, and its benefits need a >8-bit output path that a BC1 texture cannot carry.

### Cited Findings
- Jpegli decoder, as described by Google: "Support for 16-bit unsigned and 32-bit floating point output buffers"; "Non-zero DCT coefficients are dequantized to the expectation value of their respective quantization intervals assuming a Laplacian distribution of the original unquantized DCT coefficients"; "inverse DCT, chroma upsampling and color space conversions are all done in floating point precision, the conversion to integer samples happens only in the final output phase". VENDOR CLAIM, no quality numbers for the decoder — [jpegli README](https://github.com/google/jpegli)
- The jpegli paper is an encoder study: human raters compared images from MozJPEG, libjpeg-turbo and the Jpegli *encoder*; at libjpeg-turbo quality 95 Jpegli images were "54% likely to be preferred" at 2.8 bpp versus 3.8 and 3.5 bpp. It contains no measurement of decoder-side gain. MEASURED (encoder) — [jpegli paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18589)
- zenjpeg (pure-Rust jpegli port) decoder options: `.chroma_upsampling(method)` default `Triangle` ("NearestNeighbor for speed. Default matches libjpeg-turbo within max_diff <= 3"); `.dequant_bias(true)`: "f32 IDCT + Laplacian bias for max reconstruction quality", listed as "1.5-2x slower" (`SrgbF32Precise`); default IDCT is "Jpegli", with `Libjpeg` for "pixel-exact mozjpeg match". No quality numbers are given for the dequant bias or the f32 path. VENDOR CLAIM — [zenjpeg](https://github.com/imazen/zenjpeg)
- zenjpeg offers no chroma upsampling method beyond Triangle and NearestNeighbor. — [zenjpeg](https://github.com/imazen/zenjpeg)
- Quant Smooth has a "non-blurring chroma upsampling" (`UPSAMPLE_UV`, quality 6) and luma-dependent chroma (`JOINT_YUV`, quality 5); no numbers. AUTHOR CLAIM — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- jpeg2png's author investigated "better chroma upsampling" and dropped it: "too late, too small to make a real difference". AUTHOR CLAIM — [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png)
- libjpeg's fast upsampling vs fancy: "The visual impact of the sloppier method is often very small." — [libjpeg.txt](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/libjpeg-turbo/libjpeg-turbo/main/doc/libjpeg.txt)
- Dithered dequantisation as an idea: instead of the interval centre, "a random number in the interval [-Q/2; Q/2] can be added to the dequantized coefficient", turning boundary artefacts into "more pleasing 'grains'" (Wikipedia, citing Hudson et al., "JPEG-1 standard 25 years", J. Electronic Imaging 2018). Described as a look, not as a fidelity gain; no numbers. — [Wiki CA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compression_artifact)
- Project measurement: zune-jpeg 0.5.13 already uses triangle ("fancy") chroma upsampling; "Nothing cheap is left to gain there". MEASURED/inspected by the project — [XEL bench]

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: jpegli-style Laplacian-expectation dequantisation is the cheapest "principled" change in this whole survey (a per-coefficient bias towards zero, no extra pass), but it requires coefficient access and I found no number for its gain; treat as unproven. It shrinks non-zero AC coefficients, i.e. it slightly lowers contrast of fine detail, which is the opposite direction of what the project's "detail vs truth 0.95-0.97" figure asks for, although in an MSE sense it is the better estimate. Only a measurement can settle it.
- OWN INFERENCE: higher-precision decode (float IDCT, 16-bit intermediates) addresses banding in smooth gradients at >8-bit output. XEL's output is BC1, with RGB565 endpoints and 2-bit indices per 4x4 block; the encoder's quantisation is far coarser than the +-1 LSB differences between integer and float IDCT. No demonstrated gain, and none plausible after BC1. (Where precision could matter is the linear-light mip chain, which is outside this topic.)
- OWN INFERENCE: luma-guided / edge-directed chroma upsampling could only matter on 4:2:0 chunks at strongly coloured edges (red roofs, road markings, field boundaries). Two tool authors who tried a better upsampler report nothing measurable (jpeg2png: "too small to make a real difference"; Quant Smooth: no numbers). BC1 then stores colour as two endpoints per 4x4 block, which is itself coarser than 2x2 chroma subsampling. No demonstrated gain.

### Gaps
- Google's blog post "Introducing Jpegli" (opensource.googleblog.com) could not be fetched from this environment; any statement in it about 10+ bit decoding and banding is not verified here. Unverified (snippet only): "traditional JPEG coding solutions offer only 8 bit per component dynamics causing visible banding artifacts in slow gradients"; a libjpeg-turbo mailing-list thread reportedly saying Google does not claim comparable decompression performance.
- No opened source measures PSNR/SSIM of triangle vs edge-directed/luma-guided chroma upsampling, of float vs integer IDCT at 8-bit output, or of Laplacian-biased vs centre dequantisation.
- libjxl's own JPEG decode/upsampling path was not examined.

## 4. Rust availability: who exposes DCT coefficients and quantisation tables

### Takeaway
Coefficient access from Rust realistically means C FFI (`mozjpeg-sys` exposes `jpeg_read_coefficients`) or the AGPL/commercial `zenjpeg`; zune-jpeg and jpeg-decoder do not expose coefficients, though zune-jpeg's development branch exposes the quantisation tables and raw (pre-colour-conversion) component planes.

### Cited Findings
- **zune-jpeg** (XEL's decoder; `Cargo.lock` pins 0.5.13; latest on crates.io 0.5.16-rc3, updated 2026-10-19): in the `dev` branch `decoder.rs` the coefficient buffers are decoder-internal (`pub(crate)`/private `raw_coeff`); public API includes `quantization_tables()` ("in natural (row-major) coefficient order ... Together with the raw planes, this lets a caller reason about the precision the encoder kept for each component"), `raw_output()` ("exclusive raw component-output session"), `decode_into_planes`, scanline output. No public coefficient accessor was found. — [zune-jpeg src](https://github.com/etemesi254/zune-image/tree/dev/crates/zune-jpeg), [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/zune-jpeg)
- **jpeg-decoder** (image-rs, 0.3.2, updated 2025-06-21): `coefficients: Vec<Vec<i16>>` is a private field of `Decoder`; the public functions are `new`, `info`, `read_info`, `scale`, `decode`, metadata getters. No coefficient or quantisation-table accessor. — [jpeg-decoder src](https://github.com/image-rs/jpeg-decoder), [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/jpeg-decoder)
- **mozjpeg-sys** (2.2.4-beta.1; "FFI bindings for MozJPEG v4 ... built and linked statically. Requires nasm and a C compiler"): declares `jpeg_read_coefficients(cinfo) -> *mut *mut jvirt_barray_control`, `access_virt_barray` and `quantval: [u16; 64]`, i.e. full coefficient and table access through unsafe FFI. — [mozjpeg-sys src](https://github.com/kornelski/mozjpeg-sys), [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/mozjpeg-sys)
- **mozjpeg** (safe wrapper, 0.10.13): `Decompress` exposes `dct_method`, `do_fancy_upsampling`, `do_block_smoothing`, `raw()`/`read_raw_data` (raw YCbCr planes), scanline readers; no safe coefficient reader was found in `decompress.rs`. — [mozjpeg-rust src](https://github.com/ImageOptim/mozjpeg-rust)
- **turbojpeg** crate (1.5.1, updated 2026-07-25): "Fast and easy JPEG encoding, decoding and lossless transforms with TurboJPEG". — [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates/turbojpeg)
- **zenjpeg** (0.8.4, updated 2026-06-01; "Pure Rust JPEG encoder/decoder with perceptual optimizations", jpegli port; crates.io also lists `jpegli-rs` 0.12.0 by the same organisation): has a coefficient decode path, built-in `DeblockMode::{Boundary4Tap, Knusperli, Auto}`, `dequant_bias`, f32 output. Licence: "Dual-licensed: AGPL-3.0 or commercial". "Decoder API is prerelease: expect breaking changes." Baseline decode throughput 73.8 MiB/s vs 78.1 MiB/s for libjpeg-turbo ("~6% slower"). VENDOR CLAIM for the speed figures — [zenjpeg](https://github.com/imazen/zenjpeg), [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates?q=jpegli)
- **jpegli** crate (0.1.0, 2024-04-09, "Higher-level wrapper for Jpegli library", repo Szpadel/jpegli-rs): exists on crates.io; not examined further. — [crates.io](https://crates.io/api/v1/crates?q=jpegli)
- Quant Smooth and jpeg2png are C programs built on libjpeg's coefficient API; Knusperli is C++ with its own JPEG reader. — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth), [jpeg2png](https://github.com/victorvde/jpeg2png), [knusperli](https://github.com/google/knusperli)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: the "upper bound from DCT coefficients" experiment the project skipped can be run offline in the bench tool with `mozjpeg-sys` (`jpeg_read_coefficients` + `quantval`), with no change to the shipped decoder. Shipping any coefficient-domain method would mean either adding a C dependency (mozjpeg/libjpeg-turbo, needs nasm and a C compiler, and CLAUDE.md shows the project otherwise avoids external binaries), taking an AGPL/commercial crate (zenjpeg), or patching zune-jpeg.
- OWN INFERENCE: licence fit for linking into XEL depends on XEL's own licence (not checked here): jpeg2png GPLv3+, Quant Smooth LGPL-2.1, Knusperli Apache-2.0, FBCNN Apache-2.0, zenjpeg AGPL-3.0/commercial.
- OWN INFERENCE: zune-jpeg's `quantization_tables()` on the dev branch is enough for table-driven *pixel-domain* filters (what the project's variant D already does via its own `JpegInfo`), but not for quantisation-constraint projection, which needs the quantised coefficients themselves (they can be recomputed by a forward DCT of the decoded planes only approximately, because of rounding, clamping and chroma upsampling).

### Gaps
- Whether `quantization_tables()`/`raw_output()` exist in the zune-jpeg version XEL pins (0.5.13) or only in the dev branch was not checked.
- docs.rs pages for `turbojpeg` could not be fetched; whether its safe API exposes coefficients (beyond lossless transforms) is unverified. TurboJPEG's lossless-transform API with a custom filter callback is a possible route, not confirmed here.

## 5. Counter-evidence: are residual JPEG artefacts at quality ~80 below visibility, so that none of this is worth doing?

### Takeaway
The strongest evidence that nothing here pays off is the project's own measurement (deblocking <= +0.0003 SSIM, deringing <= +0.0006 SSIM against a zoom-18 reference) together with external results that cheap methods are neutral or harmful at QF 70-80; I could not open a subjective study that states at which libjpeg quality JPEG artefacts fall below one JND, so "invisible at q 80" is supported indirectly, not by a cited threshold.

### Cited Findings
- Project bench, 36 real tiles (Bing q79 4:2:0, ArcGIS q75-85, Apple q80): table-driven deblocking changes SSIM by at most +0.0003; deringing +0.0002 to +0.0006 (max +0.0019 on Bing forest), "Real, but below anything visible"; "At JPEG quality 79 to 85 there is little blocking to remove"; differences before and after BC1 agree to within 0.002. Synthetic sweep: table-driven deblock gains +0.0014 SSIM at quality 60, +0.0005 at 75 and nothing at 90. MEASURED — [XEL bench]
- At QF 80 a quantisation-constrained deblocker gains +0.00 dB / +0.0001 SSIM; TV decoding and dictionary+TV decoding lose 0.80 dB and 1.43 dB. MEASURED — [Shu-Wu 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1601.01339)
- Knusperli "hurts at Q70+" in the one Rust decoder that integrates it; its boundary filter is credited with only "+0.5 at Q90" on the vendor's scale where Q10 gives +10. VENDOR CLAIM — [zenjpeg](https://github.com/imazen/zenjpeg)
- Learned restoration gain "drops off steeply at quality 90"; at quality 80 it is still about 1.3-1.6 dB on natural-photo benchmarks. MEASURED (figure) — [QGAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09320). This is the main evidence *against* "nothing left to gain": a measurable residual exists at q 80, it is just not reachable cheaply.
- "You may not notice jpeg artifacts on the screen without zooming in, but you may notice them after printing." AUTHOR CLAIM — [quantsmooth](https://github.com/ilyakurdyukov/jpeg-quantsmooth)
- "JPEG compression artifacts blend well into photographs with detailed non-uniform textures, allowing higher compression ratios." and, on an example at Q = 25: "has little noticeable loss of detail or visible artifacts. However, once a certain threshold of compression is passed, compressed images show increasingly visible defects." (Wikipedia, illustrative example, not a controlled study) — [Wiki JPEG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG)
- JND methodology exists for this range: the JPEG AIC-3 study scales distortion levels at "0.25 JND per level" with 1 JND defined as 75 % correct detection, and places the flicker-test "visually lossless" threshold between distortion levels 2 and 4 of its ten levels; it covers JPEG among five codecs on 620x800 crops. The paper does not tabulate which libjpeg quality corresponds to 1 JND. MEASURED (method), no q-mapping — [AIC-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09501)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: three independent lines agree that *cheap* restoration has no demonstrated gain at q 75-85: the project's bench, Shu-Wu's QF 80 table (deblocking 0.00 dB, TV negative), and zenjpeg's "hurts at Q70+". Techniques with NO demonstrated gain at this quality level: boundary deblocking (integer or quantisation-aware), Knusperli, TV/TGV quantisation-constrained decoding (jpeg2png), dictionary+TV decoding, libjpeg block smoothing (not applicable at all), better-than-triangle chroma upsampling, float/high-precision IDCT at 8-bit output, dithered dequantisation, and Quant Smooth (no numbers published at any quality).
- OWN INFERENCE: techniques WITH demonstrated gain at QF 80 (vs the uncompressed original, natural photos): BM3D with known error variance (+0.94 dB), WNNM (+1.21 dB), DTPD (+1.69 dB), low-rank soft decoding (+2.12 dB), QGAC (about +1.3 to +1.6 dB). All cost seconds to minutes per 16.8 MP tile on CPU. None fits "a few ms to low tens of ms"; none was tested on aerial imagery or after BC1.
- OWN INFERENCE: in XEL's pipeline the viewer almost never sees level 0 at 1:1 without further filtering: tiles pass a 2x2 linear-light mip chain, BC1 (4x4 blocks, RGB565 endpoints), then trilinear/anisotropic sampling on terrain. 8x8 JPEG block edges at q 80 are attenuated by each stage, and the project measured that BC1 neither removes nor adds to the differences (within 0.002 SSIM). The remaining error versus ground truth is dominated by resolution and resampling, not by JPEG quantisation.
- OWN INFERENCE (what would be worth measuring, if anything): the skipped "upper bound" experiment. Use `mozjpeg-sys` coefficients in the bench tool to run (a) jpegli-style Laplacian-expectation dequantisation and (b) a full quantisation-constraint method (Quant Smooth via its C library) on the existing 36 tiles. If even (b) stays below about +0.002 SSIM against the zoom-18 truth, the whole topic can be closed with a measurement instead of an argument.

### Gaps
- No opened source states the libjpeg quality at which JPEG becomes visually lossless / below 1 JND. Unverified (snippet only, not opened): Ullah et al., "Subjective Assessment of Objective Image Quality Metrics Range Guaranteeing Visually Lossless Compression", Sensors 23(3):1297, 2023 (flicker test, 25 participants); Testolina et al., "JPEG AIC-3 Dataset: Towards Defining the High Quality to Nearly Visually Lossless Quality Range" (EPFL record). MDPI and the EPFL record could not be fetched here.
- No study of JPEG artefact visibility specifically in aerial/satellite imagery, or under texture minification/BC1, was found.
- The claim that q 80 is "commonly near-transparent" is general knowledge with no opened source; do not cite it as established.
