## 1. Better mip downsampling than box (Wronski, Castaño, NVTT)

### Takeaway
Two graphics experts say box is a poor downsampling filter and publish alternatives (Wronski: an 8-bilinear-tap filter with exact weights; Castaño: Kaiser/Lanczos as production standard, Magic Kernel Sharp as "promising"), but neither gives a numeric measurement, and none of the opened sources tests distant terrain or orthophotos. The evidence is frequency-response plots, animations and side-by-side images.

### Cited Findings
- [OPENED] Bart Wronski, "Fast, GPU friendly, antialiasing downsampling filter", 7 March 2022. BLOG OPINION with derivation. Exact filter (GLSL, `uv` centred between 4 source pixels): four taps of weight `0.37487566` at offsets `(±0.75777, ±0.75777)` and four taps of weight `-0.12487566` at `(±2.907, 0)` and `(0, ±2.907)` source pixels. Derived from a least-squares-optimal 8-tap 1D filter, outer product to 8x8, weights below 0.02 zeroed (32-tap "cross"), re-optimised, then each same-sign 2x2 group replaced by one bilinear tap. — [Wronski 2022](https://bartwronski.com/2022/03/07/fast-gpu-friendly-antialiasing-downsampling-filter/)
- Same post, his claims: "This filter behaves way better than a bilinear one under motion, aliases less, is sharper, and is not that computationally heavy"; "almost all aliasing is gone – except for some of diagonal edges"; "it is purely superior to box downsample – and not sacrificing any sharpness". Evidence is a synthetic GIF, a shadertoy recording and frequency-response plots; no metric. The 1D prototype has a ripple ("some frequencies will be boosted / sharpened"); he argues this does not accumulate over 10 mip levels because each level's boost maps to different original frequencies. BLOG OPINION. — [Wronski 2022](https://bartwronski.com/2022/03/07/fast-gpu-friendly-antialiasing-downsampling-filter/)
- Same post, on mipmaps specifically: "For sure it is better than a bilinear/box filter. If you create mip-maps on the fly / in runtime ... probably yes. But counter-intuitively, a filter that does some more sharpening or even lets through some aliasing might be actually pretty good and desireable for mipmaps", because the mip will be bilinearly upsampled for trilinear filtering. BLOG OPINION. — [Wronski 2022](https://bartwronski.com/2022/03/07/fast-gpu-friendly-antialiasing-downsampling-filter/)
- Same post: an 8x8 separable filter "might be ok for 'separable' downsampling, which is often a good idea on CPU or DSP, but not necessarily on the GPU". BLOG OPINION. — [Wronski 2022](https://bartwronski.com/2022/03/07/fast-gpu-friendly-antialiasing-downsampling-filter/)
- [OPENED] Bart Wronski, "Bilinear down/upsampling, aligning pixel grids, and that infamous GPU half pixel offset", 15 Feb 2021. For 2x reduction "box" and "bilinear" downsampling are the same weights; even-length filters keep the pixel-corner alignment GPUs use, odd filters such as `[0.25 0.5 0.25]` shift the grid by half a pixel relative to it; bilinear down/upsampling is "inherently low quality and I don't recommend using it when the quality matters more than simplicity / performance". BLOG OPINION with worked examples. — [Wronski 2021](https://bartwronski.com/2021/02/15/bilinear-down-upsampling-pixel-grids-and-that-half-pixel-offset/)
- [OPENED] Ignacio Castaño, "Runtime Mipmap Generation", 18 Feb 2026. Windowed sinc (Lanczos, Kaiser) "was my choice in the NVIDIA Texture Tools, and it is also what most production pipelines still use today"; Wronski and Bloom "suggest designing filter weights with the knowledge that mipmaps will be reconstructed using bilinear filtering"; a client "reported great results using the magic kernel sharp filter"; "I haven't done a formal evaluation yet, but the initial results look promising." His Magic Kernel Sharp implementation needs "36 texture samples per thread (or 25 relying on bilinear filtering for the central taps)" and its weights alternate in sign. BLOG OPINION with image comparisons (box vs Magic Kernel Sharp; three.js vs Basis vs spark.js: "preserves the brightness of the stitching in the helmet without loss in the high frequency details"). — [Castaño 2026](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/2026/02/runtime-mipmap-generation/)
- Same post: "Preserving energy across mip levels is one of the most important factors in the visual quality of mipmaps"; gamma-space filtering causes "a general darkening that causes fine details to fade out too quickly". Alpha-coverage preservation concerns alpha-tested textures (foliage); ortho textures are opaque, so it does not apply (last clause OWN INFERENCE). BLOG OPINION. — [Castaño 2026](https://www.ludicon.com/castano/blog/2026/02/runtime-mipmap-generation/)
- [OPENED] NVIDIA Texture Tools API documentation (wiki, Castaño). `MipmapFilter_Box` "It's the default option and good choice for most cases. It's also much faster than the other filters." `MipmapFilter_Triangle`: "larger width and thus produces blurrier results than the box filter". `MipmapFilter_Kaiser`: "generally considered the best choice for downsampling filters, but ... the resulting images could suffer from ringing artifacts or the result may not be as sharp as desired"; defaults `setKaiserParameters(3.0f, 4.0f, 1.0f)` (width, alpha, stretch); "Values above 5 are not recommended." Border: NVTT assumes mirror wrap by default; the wrap mode should be set explicitly. DOCUMENTATION. — [NVTT ApiDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/castano/nvidia-texture-tools/ApiDocumentation.md)
- X-Plane reads the file's stored mip levels directly as its reduced-resolution loads: "Since DDS is already pre-minified ... the sim can simply load a lower res version" and LOAD_CENTER makes X-Plane "reduce the resolution of the texture as you fly away from that point". LAMINAR STATEMENT (Ben Supnik, 17 March 2011). — [Three Things You Need for Fast Orthophotos](https://developer.x-plane.com/2011/03/three-things-you-need-for-fast-orthophotos/)
- A third-party streamer measured which level X-Plane 12 reads by distance: with a 3840 px screen at 62° FOV, a 2048 px, 6.2 km ZL16 texture "was read at full detail from 25.8 km (to its centre), at level 1 from 48.4 km, level 2 from 93 km and level 3 from 180 km"; model: level n is wanted while `x < 2^n k`, x = distance to the near edge in the texture's ground pixels, k about 7500 to 8200 on that screen. MEASURED by that project (code comment, single setup, not independently checked). — [OrthoStreamXP PagingModel.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/Paging/PagingModel.cs)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: The mip levels XEarthLayer writes matter twice: the GPU samples them (trilinear/AF), and X-Plane's pager loads only levels >= n for textures beyond a distance. With OrthoStreamXP's k ≈ 8000 and a 4096 px ZL16 tile about 6.2 km wide, level 0 would be resident only within roughly 8000 x 6200 / 4096 ≈ 12 km of the tile's near edge on a 3840 px screen (less on smaller screens, since k scaled with screen width in their fit). Beyond that the best available image is level 1 or coarser. A better filter for levels 1..n therefore changes most of the visible terrain area, while level 0 changes only the near field.
- OWN INFERENCE (arithmetic, from the prior report): box has per-axis response cos(pi f); it attenuates in-band detail (0.71 at f = 0.25 cycles/texel) and leaks 38 % at f = 0.375. A Kaiser/Lanczos or Wronski-type filter is flatter in the passband and steeper in the stopband, so distant terrain should keep more mid-frequency contrast and alias less. Nobody in the opened sources measured this on terrain.
- OWN INFERENCE on cost: on a CPU, Wronski's 8 bilinear taps expand to 32 source texels per output texel (his own "32 tap cross filter") against 4 for box; NVTT's default Kaiser (width 3) done separably needs about 12 taps per axis for a 2x reduction. Level 1 dominates (three quarters of all output texels of levels 1..n). Expect a multiple of the box time (order 5x to 10x unvectorised), i.e. tens of milliseconds per tile instead of 5 to 14 ms; this must be measured, no source gives a CPU timing.
- OWN INFERENCE: negative-lobe filters ring at hard edges (NVTT warning) and can overshoot below 0 or above 255; filtering must stay in linear light and clamp. Ringing on orthophotos (roof edges, roads) at levels 1..n is the risk to test.
- OWN INFERENCE: any kernel wider than 2x2 needs texels outside the 4096 tile at its border; see section 6.

### Gaps
- No opened source gives a numeric quality comparison (PSNR/SSIM/flicker) of box vs Kaiser/Lanczos/Wronski for mip chains, and none for terrain at grazing angles.
- No CPU timing for Kaiser or the Wronski filter on a 4096 chain was found. NVTT says only "much faster" for box.
- Wronski's "Practical Gaussian filtering" and "Exposure fusion"/"Progressive image stylisation" posts were not opened; the 2022 post only links the exposure-fusion post as its neighbour.
- Jonathan Blow's "Inner Product" mipmapping articles (cited by Castaño) and Charles Bloom's posts were not opened.

## 2. Lower mips from the provider's lower zoom; supersampled level 0 from one zoom higher

### Takeaway
Building mip level n from the provider's zoom-minus-n tiles is established practice in both comparable streamers (AutoOrtho, OrthoStreamXP), but they do it to save downloads, not for quality, and OrthoStreamXP notes that Bing's coarse zooms are different imagery. Building a texture from one zoom finer and box-reducing it is a shipped option in OrthoStreamXP; no opened source measures whether a supersampled JPEG source gives a better level 0.

### Cited Findings
- AutoOrtho builds mip level m from provider tiles at `zoom - m`: `get_img` sets `zoom = self.zoom - mipmap` and creates chunks at that zoom (`chunks_per_row = 16 >> mipmap`); class constants `min_zoom = 12`, `max_mipmap = 4`; requests for levels above 4 fetch level 4, and still smaller levels are made by halving (`img.reduce_2()` in `pydds.py`). SOURCE CODE READ. — [AutoOrtho getortho.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kubilus1/autoortho/main/autoortho/getortho.py); [AutoOrtho pydds.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kubilus1/autoortho/main/autoortho/pydds.py)
- AutoOrtho fallback: when a chunk is not ready in time, `get_best_chunk` looks for a cached chunk of a coarser zoom (up to mip 4) and scales it up ("backup chunk"). SOURCE CODE READ. — [AutoOrtho getortho.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/kubilus1/autoortho/main/autoortho/getortho.py)
- OrthoStreamXP does the same per level: "Real data is fetched at exactly the zoom the level needs (level 3 of a ZL16 texture is four ZL13 tiles, not 256 ZL16 tiles)". Once level 0 is complete it is "saved as a normal cached DDS", whose smaller levels are "2x2 averages in linear light". SOURCE CODE READ (comments). — [TextureStreamer.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/Streaming/TextureStreamer.cs); [Dds.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/Dds/Dds.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP on coarse provider zooms: a missing tile is made from a coarser one but "Never coarser than the base's zoom 13, Bing's coarser zooms being other imagery." SOURCE CODE READ (comment). — [TextureBuilder.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/TextureBuilder.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP presets decouple download zoom from file zoom: "Balanced 15/16, Sharp 16/17, Ultra 17/18, Extra 17/19, whatever the zoom in the package's file names"; "Ultra at Medium: ZL16 4096 px from zoom 17", i.e. a 4096 px texture reduced from the next finer zoom. Reduction is "Box-halving while at least 2x too large, then bicubic for whatever ratio remains", done "tile by tile as they arrive (exact box filter, no seams)". The stated purpose of Medium/Low is graphics memory ("to fit a smaller graphics card from the same imagery"), not image quality. SOURCE CODE READ and PRODUCT CLAIM. — [TextureQuality.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/TextureQuality.cs); [Images.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/Images.cs); [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/main/README.md)
- GDAL `gdaladdo`: default resampling is `nearest`, "generally not appropriate if sub-pixel accuracy is desired"; `average` is a weighted average of contributing pixels; `gauss` "can lead to better results than simple averaging in e.g case of sharp edges with high contrast or noisy patterns"; `cubic`, `cubicspline`, `lanczos` are listed without a recommendation; the documentation's own examples use `-r average` and `-r cubic`. DOCUMENTATION. — [gdaladdo.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OSGeo/gdal/master/doc/source/programs/gdaladdo.rst)
- GDAL COG driver: `OVERVIEW_RESAMPLING` defaults to CUBIC for non-paletted images ("For paletted images, NEAREST is used by default, otherwise it is CUBIC"). DOCUMENTATION. — [cog.rst](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OSGeo/gdal/master/doc/source/drivers/raster/cog.rst)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: provider lower-zoom tiles as lower mips trade one risk for another. In favour: the provider resampled from the original imagery, and four 256 px JPEGs replace 256. Against: the provider's filter is unknown, the result is JPEG-compressed again, and the coarse zoom can be a different acquisition (OrthoStreamXP's remark about Bing below zoom 13), so content, colour or registration may change between mip levels; trilinear filtering would then blend two different pictures. For XEarthLayer, which already holds all 256 chunks when it encodes, this buys no quality that a good own filter does not also give. It is a download-saving technique.
- OWN INFERENCE: a level 0 reduced from the next finer zoom averages four decoded JPEG pixels per texel, so 8x8 block artefacts shrink to 4x4 and are averaged down, and the reduction filter is under XEarthLayer's control instead of the provider's. Whether the gain is visible depends on how the provider made its own zoom 16 from the same master. Cost: 1024 chunk downloads instead of 256 per tile and 4x the decode work. Unmeasured.
- OWN INFERENCE: GDAL's defaults (cubic for COG overviews, average in examples) show that mapping practice does not treat plain averaging as the only acceptable overview filter, but GDAL gives no orthophoto-specific guidance in the two files read.

### Gaps
- No opened source measures JPEG artefacts or antialiasing of a supersampled tile source ("retina tiles", overzoom) against the native zoom. No tile-server documentation on this was opened.
- Whether Bing/Google zoom levels are resampled from the same master at every zoom is not documented in anything opened.

## 3. Serving 8192x8192 DDS from chunk zoom 17

### Takeaway
No Laminar statement on an X-Plane 12 texture size limit was found; the only limit Laminar states in the opened pages is 4096x4096 from 2012 (X-Plane 10). A third-party X-Plane 12 streamer ships 8192 px terrain textures and reports X-Plane reading them in a test flight, on condition that the `.ter` LOAD_CENTER pixel size is rewritten to match. Laminar says the texture slider and the VRAM pager lower every scenery texture, orthophotos included.

### Cited Findings
- "Texture size limit bumped up to 4096 x 4096 max. (The max panel size will remain 2048 x 2048.)" LAMINAR STATEMENT (Ben Supnik, March 2012, X-Plane 10). — [Conditionals, Shadows, Big Textures, Etc.](https://developer.x-plane.com/2012/03/conditionals-shadows-big-textures-etc/)
- Earlier limits: "The maximum single texture size in X-Plane 8 is 1024×1024, and in X-Plane 9 it is 2048×2048." LAMINAR STATEMENT (Supnik, 6 Feb 2008). — [The Limits of Orthophotos and Meshes in X-Plane](https://developer.x-plane.com/2008/02/the-limits-of-orthophotos-and-meshes-in-x-plane/)
- LOAD_CENTER takes the texture size in pixels: "By also specifying the approximate terrain size when placed (in meters) and textures size (in pixels), X-Plane will load the texture with variable resolution based on the general distance from the user to the texture"; example `LOAD_CENTER 42.70321 -72.34234 4000 1024`. DOCUMENTATION (Laminar, last updated 28 June 2018). — [.ter file format specification](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/terrain-type-ter-file-format-specification/)
- Ortho4XP writes `LOAD_CENTER <lat> <lon> <size_m> 4096` into every ortho `.ter`. SOURCE CODE READ. — [Ortho4XP O4_DSF_Utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_DSF_Utils.py)
- OrthoStreamXP builds up to 8192 px (`MaxSize = 8192`): "ZL16 textures: Balanced 2048, Sharp 4096, Ultra and Extra 8192; ZL18 textures: Balanced 1024, Sharp 2048, Ultra 4096, Extra 8192", and serves `.ter` files "whose LOAD_CENTER pixel size matches the texture size", rewritten in memory. SOURCE CODE READ. — [TextureQuality.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/TextureQuality.cs); [TerrainDefinitions.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/FileSystem/TerrainDefinitions.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP reports X-Plane 12 reading such textures: "in the test flight of 17 Nov every such read of the 8192 px ZL16 and ZL18 textures wanted their 512 px level, 100-200 km out where the distance asks for 64 px, and read nothing more of 95 % of them." MEASURED by that project (code comment). — [MipWarmer.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/Prefetch/MipWarmer.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP release notes v1.5.0: "Twice as sharp, from the same downloads ... Balanced 2048 px, Sharp 4096 px, and Ultra and Extra 8192 px"; "The bigger textures take more room in the cache and more graphics memory ... If X-Plane stutters or turns blurry, set the preset's Resolution to Medium". PRODUCT CLAIM. — [release-notes/v1.5.0.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/release-notes/v1.5.0.md)
- OrthoStreamXP also warns that file size must stay fixed: "The file always has its full size, header and every level: a smaller texture, a short read or an error would make X-Plane treat the texture as final." SOURCE CODE READ (comment). — [TextureStreamer.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/Streaming/TextureStreamer.cs)
- Texture slider and pager in X-Plane 12: "The texture slider in the UI sets the maximum texture resolution X-Plane will attempt ... But X-Plane will further lower res until it fits – X-Plane will not use system memory as backup texture memory". LAMINAR STATEMENT (Supnik, 14 Sept 2022). — [X-Plane 12 Early Access Is Here](https://developer.x-plane.com/2022/09/x-plane-12-early-access-is-here/)
- Replies in the comment thread of Laminar's VRAM post (11.50 era; reply author names were not captured by my text extraction, the replies speak for the developers): "When you turn the texture slider down one notch, _everything_ that is eligible to change resolutions does change resolution ... (all scenery, orthos and not, ...) go down a notch"; "If we run out of VRAM, we will turn down the 'dynamic adjust'"; "Non-orthos are down-res'd when possible when they are proven to be far from the user; all scenery textures are further affected by the dynamic res adjustment"; "Every time we drop a textures resolution in half it loses ~77% of its size". LAMINAR STATEMENT (comment replies, author not verified). — [Fighting blurry textures](https://developer.x-plane.com/2020/05/fighting-blurry-textures/)
- VRAM cost scales with dimensions and the mip chain adds one third ("The 4/3 term is due to mipmapping"); DXT1 is 4 bits per pixel. LAMINAR STATEMENT (Supnik 2011, 2012). — [Image Size on Disk and VRAM](https://developer.x-plane.com/2011/05/image-size-on-disk-and-vram/); [DDS Revisited In X-Plane 10](https://developer.x-plane.com/2012/01/dds-revisited-in-x-plane-10/)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: an 8192 BC1 tile is 4x the bytes of a 4096 one (about 42.7 MiB with mips against 10.66 MiB), 4x the downloads (1024 chunks) and 4x the encode time, and touches XEarthLayer's memory design (`M_MMAP_THRESHOLD`, cache budgets, pinned-handle ceiling are all sized around 10.66 MiB tiles).
- OWN INFERENCE: if X-Plane lowers the resolution (slider below maximum or VRAM pressure), an 8192 file is shown from its level 1, which is a 4096 image reduced from zoom 17: the supersampled level 0 of section 2, obtained as a side effect. So 8192 serving degrades gracefully into "supersampled 4096".
- OWN INFERENCE: XEarthLayer would have to rewrite LOAD_CENTER's pixel size in the `.ter` files it passes through (OrthoStreamXP does this; it implies the value matters to X-Plane's pager). Serving 8192 with LOAD_CENTER still saying 4096 is untested in the opened sources.
- OWN INFERENCE: the near-field benefit is real resolution (1.2 m instead of 2.4 m per texel), which is the only technique in this note that adds information rather than preserving it.

### Gaps
- No Laminar statement on a maximum texture size for X-Plane 12 was found (developer.x-plane.com searches for "8192", "8K", "max texture size", "4096", "texture resolution"; a web search returned no such statement either). The 8192 evidence is one third-party project, not independently verified here.
- What X-Plane does when LOAD_CENTER's pixel size disagrees with the file is not documented in anything opened.
- www.x-plane.com release notes (which might state texture limits) are not reachable from this environment.

## 4. X-Plane detail mechanisms: decals and normal maps in .ter files

### Takeaway
Decals are Laminar's documented mechanism for close-range detail and are allowed in `.ter` files; Ortho4XP can already emit one library decal (off by default), and an on-the-fly `.ter` rewrite in the mounted filesystem is proven practice (OrthoStreamXP does it). Practitioner opinion on the look over orthophotos is split: Ortho4XP's author says it helps at very low altitude and distracts higher up; OrthoStreamXP's author calls the full-strength effect "noise" and ships it attenuated to 30 %.

### Cited Findings
- "Decals are overlay textures that sit on top of a regular albedo (day-time) texture to add high-frequency, high-res detail to an existing texture." "as you move away from the decal, the decal blurs into 'no effect'. This means that you can generally apply decals and not worry about the effect on the far-away view." "Decals are allowed in any file that uses the Standard Shader, including .ter, .pol, and .net files." Greyscale decals blend "similar to Photoshop's 'hard light' mode". DOCUMENTATION (Laminar, last updated 30 May 2014). — [Using Decals to Add Detail To Scenery](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)
- Same article, scale advice from the author's tests: if the scale ratio between successive elements "exceeds a value in the region of 100 – 150, it is difficult to make them look integrated"; "if a 10 x 10 m decal is applied directly to a 4000 x 4000 m albedo, the effect will probably not be good, since the scale ratio is very large: 400.0"; an intermediate-scale normal map (`NORMAL_TEX 6.0 ...`) helps bind them; "a looser, more impressionistic approach can be as – or more – effective." DOCUMENTATION with author's opinion. — [Using Decals to Add Detail To Scenery](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)
- Commands and cost: "The decal stage blends a higher resolution repeating texture into a base texture to create the illusion of high resolution. Because the decal is part of the shading process (and not an additional layer on top of the terrain), the performance impact is quite low." "Shaders can use up to two decal commands"; a `.dcl` file with two decal directives uses the whole budget. Commands: `DECAL`, `DECAL_RGBA`, `DECAL_KEYED`, `DECAL_PARAMS`, `DECAL_PARAMS_PROJ` (repeats every scale_x/scale_y metres, independent of the albedo), `DECAL_LIB [file path or library path]`; from X-Plane 12.1.0 also `NORMAL_DECAL`, `NORMAL_DECAL_KEYED`, `NORMAL_DECAL_PARAMS`, `NORMAL_DECAL_PARAMS_PROJ`. Normal map commands take a ratio ("a ratio of 4.0 means the normal map repeats 4 times for every tile of the albedo texture") and have no-wrap variants for `.ter`/`.pol`. DOCUMENTATION (Laminar, last updated 10 Oct 2024). — [Standard Shading Options for X-Plane Scenery](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/standard-shading-options-for-x-plane-10-scenery/)
- The older `.ter` specification lists BASE_TEX(_NOWRAP), LIT_TEX, BORDER_TEX, COMPOSITE_TEX, PROJECTED, AUTO_VARY, LOAD_CENTER and others; compositing: "[X-Plane 9] As of X-Plane 9, compositing is deprecated and not recommended." It does not list decal or normal commands (those are in the Standard Shading article). DOCUMENTATION. — [.ter file format specification](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/terrain-type-ter-file-format-specification/)
- What Ortho4XP writes: `A 800 TERRAIN`, `LOAD_CENTER ... 4096`, `BASE_TEX_NOWRAP ../textures/<file>`, for water `WATER_COLOR_MASK` or `BORDER_TEX` (with `LOAD_CENTER_BORDER`), `WET` or `NO_ALPHA`, `NO_SHADOW` when `terrain_casts_shadows` is off, and, if `use_decal_on_terrain` is set and the triangle is not inland water, `DECAL_LIB lib/g10/decals/maquify_2_green_key.dcl`. No NORMAL_TEX is written; `normal_map_strength` scales the mesh normals in the DSF. SOURCE CODE READ. — [Ortho4XP O4_DSF_Utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_DSF_Utils.py)
- Ortho4XP option text: `use_decal_on_terrain`, default `False`: "Terrain files for all but water triangles will contain the maquify_1_green_key.dcl decal directive. The effect is noticeable at very low altitude and helps to overcome the orthophoto blur at such levels. Can be slightly distracting at higher altitude." (The hint names `maquify_1`, the code writes `maquify_2`.) SOURCE CODE READ, author's opinion. — [Ortho4XP O4_Config_Utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)
- OrthoStreamXP takes the opposite view and weakens the decal: "X-Plane draws its own procedural grass, shrubs and dirt over the ground up close. On sharp aerial photos this looks like noise: dark speckles and pale stony patches that the real imagery does not have." v1.2.0 turned it off by default; v1.5.1: "Ground detail starts at 30 % ... At 30 %, X-Plane's grass and dirt pattern is faint, and the photos stay clean and sharp." User guide: "This helps X-Plane's blurry default textures, but on sharp aerial photos it looks like noise ... The photo scenery packages ask for it in every photo tile". PRODUCT CLAIM / developer opinion. — [release-notes/v1.2.0.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/release-notes/v1.2.0.md); [release-notes/v1.5.1.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/release-notes/v1.5.1.md); [docs/settings.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/docs/settings.md)
- How OrthoStreamXP does it, in the mounted filesystem without touching packages: at 100 % the `DECAL_LIB` line stays; at 0 % decal lines are removed; in between "the library's own decal lines are written into the definition in its place, pointing at faded copies of its textures that the mount serves from memory". "The decals modulate the ground's colours around mid-grey (2x modulation: 128 leaves it as it is), so a decal texture faded towards grey is the same decal, weaker." Its generated definitions use `lib/g10/decals/maquify_2_green_key.dcl`. SOURCE CODE READ. — [TerrainDecals.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/FileSystem/TerrainDecals.cs); [TerrainDefinitions.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Scenery/FileSystem/TerrainDefinitions.cs)
- Laminar on the default-scenery trade-off: Austin Meyer (quoted by Supnik) called orthophotos "blurry. And pixelated"; Supnik: "orthophotos can look very good when they are combined with 3-d in a correlated way". LAMINAR STATEMENT (6 Oct 2010). — [Orthophotos Are Not Going Away](https://developer.x-plane.com/2010/10/orthophotos-are-not-going-away/)
- WorldEditor 2.6: "Terrain objects are designed to interface with orthophotos and create small scale 3D details." LAMINAR STATEMENT (Aug 2024). — [World Editor 2.6r1 Is Available](https://developer.x-plane.com/2024/08/world-editor-2-6r1-is-available/)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: XEarthLayer can add, remove or attenuate a decal by rewriting `.ter` text as it passes through the union filesystem; no texture pixels and no DSF change are needed, and OrthoStreamXP is a working precedent for the mechanism (rewrite `.ter`, serve derived decal PNGs from a virtual folder). The `.ter` files are tiny text files, but XEarthLayer's scenery index and ortho union index key on `.ter` names and mtimes, so a rewrite must not change names or sizes reported in ways those caches depend on (project-specific, to be checked).
- OWN INFERENCE: the two practitioner opinions agree on the physics and differ on taste: the library decal is a generic grass/dirt pattern keyed to green, so it adds plausible grain over fields and implausible speckle over roofs, roads and water-adjacent land. A strength control (as OrthoStreamXP does) is the low-risk form. A decal designed for orthophotos (neutral luminance grain, `DECAL_PARAMS_PROJ` at a fixed metre scale) is possible by the documentation but nobody in the opened sources has published one.
- OWN INFERENCE: Laminar's scale rule (ratio between successive elements below about 100 to 150) matters here: a ZL16 ortho tile is about 6 km wide; a 10 to 60 m decal directly on it is a ratio of 100 to 600, the range the article says integrates badly without an intermediate layer.
- OWN INFERENCE: decals are the only mechanism in this note that adds detail below the 2.4 m texel; they add invented detail, not information, and they fade with distance by construction, so they cannot cause the far-field flicker that sharpening risks.

## 5. What comparable products do

### Takeaway
OrthoStreamXP's published source shows the widest set of image techniques: linear-light box mips, an unsharp mask before the mip chain, fixed contrast, exposure and seasonal colouring, 8192 px textures, decal attenuation, per-level fetching and a measured paging model. AutoOrtho does per-level fetching from lower zooms with halving below level 4 and coarser-zoom fallbacks. Nothing from Asobo/Microsoft or Google was opened.

### Cited Findings
- OrthoStreamXP mip chain: "its mip chain (2x2 averages in linear light, MipFilter)". SOURCE CODE READ (comment). — [Dds.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/Dds/Dds.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP sharpening: "an unsharp mask on the brightness (a Gaussian of 1 px), its change added to red, green and blue alike so edges get no colour fringes", applied "before its smaller levels and BC1"; 100 % = amount 1.5 for wide-area textures (below ZL18), 60 % for airport textures; "roofs, field edges and tree crowns crisper, no visible halos (the samples of 2026-11-18)". SOURCE CODE READ; the "no visible halos" judgement is the developer's own on still samples. — [Sharpening.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/Sharpening.cs)
- OrthoStreamXP release notes v1.5.0: "Each new texture is sharpened a little as it is built ... The photos also get a little more contrast as X-Plane reads them, because aerial photos are often hazy. Both are always on and have no setting." README: seasonal colours from the simulated date and "NASA's satellite record of how green each place really was"; "Exposure and a live preview — tone down photos that look too bright next to X-Plane's own scenery"; "Imagery varies by location: quality, capture date, colours, and transitions between imagery sources may differ." PRODUCT CLAIM. — [release-notes/v1.5.0.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/release-notes/v1.5.0.md); [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/main/README.md)

PRODUCT CLAIM. — [release-notes/v1.4.0.md](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/release-notes/v1.4.0.md)

- OWN INFERENCE: X-Plane's LOAD_CENTER paging is a distance rule with the same purpose as Cesium's screen-space-error rule; the streamer cannot change it except through the LOAD_CENTER size fields.

## 6. Seams and consistency between tiles and acquisitions

### Takeaway
Laminar's own rule for tiling orthophotos is edge clamping (`BASE_TEX_NOWRAP`), which Ortho4XP uses; the 2x2 box filter is seam-safe because it never reads across a tile edge, whereas any wider mip filter needs a border policy. Colour balancing across mosaics is an active remote-sensing research field (histogram matching, radiometric normalisation), but no opened source describes cheap per-tile correction as standard practice for streamed tiles.

### Cited Findings
- "You will need the no-wrapping tex commands when using orthophotos that tile; they guarantee that filtering does not introduce artifacts across terrain borders." With wrapping off, "the edge color is extended indefinitely." DOCUMENTATION (Laminar). — [.ter file format specification](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/terrain-type-ter-file-format-specification/)
- "The 'no-wrap' variants provide edge clamping to avoid bleeding from one side of an orthophoto to another; they are only available for .ter and .pol files." DOCUMENTATION (Laminar, 2024). — [Standard Shading Options](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/standard-shading-options-for-x-plane-10-scenery/)
- Ortho4XP writes `BASE_TEX_NOWRAP` for every ortho texture. SOURCE CODE READ. — [Ortho4XP O4_DSF_Utils.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_DSF_Utils.py)
- NVTT: filters near the border "usually sample outside of the texture. By default, NVTT assumes the texture wrapping mode is to mirror ... better results can be achieved by explicitly specifying the desired wrapping mode." DOCUMENTATION. — [NVTT ApiDocumentation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/wiki/castano/nvidia-texture-tools/ApiDocumentation.md)
- OrthoStreamXP reduces "tile by tile as they arrive (exact box filter, no seams)". SOURCE CODE READ (comment). — [TextureBuilder.cs](https://github.com/flysimdev/OrthoStreamXP/blob/main/src/OrthoStreamXP/Textures/TextureBuilder.cs)
- Remote-sensing literature, abstracts only: "Due to different seasons, illumination, and atmospheric conditions, the photometric of the acquired image varies greatly, which leads to obvious stitching seams at the edges of the mosaic image. Traditional methods can be divided into two categories, one is absolute radiation correction and the other is relative radiation normalization." (Zuo, Li, Zhang, "A Nerf-Based Color Consistency Method for Remote Sensing Images", arXiv 2411.05557, 8 Nov 2024, IGARSS 2023; MEASURED claims are the authors', abstract only read.) — [arXiv:2411.05557](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05557)
- Relative radiometric normalisation by histogram matching with change-robust selection of a "no-change set" (HM-RRN-MoG) "achieves the best performance" among the authors' variants and is "robust against clouds/fogs/changes" (Liu et al., "Auto robust relative radiometric normalization via latent change noise modelling", arXiv 2111.12406, 24 Nov 2021; abstract only read). — [arXiv:2111.12406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.12406)
- Laminar on the problem as users see it (Austin Meyer quoted by Supnik): orthophotos "with a magenta or purple tint. And with big blurry shears right through the middle of them when they fall between offset satellite passes." LAMINAR STATEMENT (2010). — [Orthophotos Are Not Going Away](https://developer.x-plane.com/2010/10/orthophotos-are-not-going-away/)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: with a 2x2 box filter every mip texel covers an aligned block inside its own tile, so adjacent tiles' mip levels stay consistent at the shared edge without neighbour data. A Kaiser, Lanczos, Magic Kernel or Wronski filter reads 3 to 6 source texels beyond the edge at level 1 (and proportionally more source area at each further level if built progressively). Options: clamp or mirror at the tile edge (cheap, slightly wrong along a strip a few texels wide at each level, on both sides of every tile border), or fetch a one-chunk ring of neighbours (68 more chunks per tile, about 27 % more downloads, mostly shared with neighbours through the chunk cache). Clamping matches what the GPU does with `BASE_TEX_NOWRAP`, so clamp is the consistent default; whether the strip is visible must be tested.
- OWN INFERENCE: the published colour-balancing methods need overlap regions or a reference image and solve a global problem; a streamer sees one tile at a time. A per-tile correction computed from that tile alone (histogram stretch, grey-world) changes neighbouring tiles differently and can create seams at 4096 px tile borders where the provider had none. Only corrections that are identical for all tiles (a fixed tone curve, as OrthoStreamXP's fixed contrast and exposure) are seam-safe by construction.
- OWN INFERENCE: acquisition seams inside a tile (the "shears" Meyer describes) are in the provider's mosaic and cannot be removed cheaply without the reference data the literature assumes.

### Gaps
- Only two arXiv abstracts were read; no full paper and no survey of mosaic colour balancing ("dodging", Wallis filter) was opened. arXiv's own search returned no hits for "mosaic color balancing remote sensing"; the two papers came from a web search.
- No source on what Bing or Google do to balance their mosaics was opened.
- No measurement of mip-level seams at tile borders with wide filters under clamp-to-edge was found.

## 7. Anisotropy-aware or ripmap-style prefiltering

### Takeaway
Not available to XEarthLayer: the DDS carries one isotropic mip chain, and anisotropic filtering is done by the GPU with an implementation-defined sample pattern, so the only lever is the quality of the isotropic levels.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia describes the trade-off of choosing a mip level for a surface at a steep angle (higher resolution aliases in one direction, lower blurs) and states that anisotropic filtering addresses it by sampling a non-isotropic footprint. Overview. — [Wikipedia, Mipmap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mipmap)
- The prior report (`/home/user/xearthlayer/reports/Texturschärfen in Bewegung.md`, question 1) documents from the Khronos/Vulkan texts that AF takes N samples along the long axis with the mip level chosen from the short axis and that the method is "implementation dependent". DOCUMENTATION, as cited there; not re-opened in this session.
- X-Plane's `.ter` texture commands name one texture file per role (base, lit, border, normal, decal); no command supplies separate anisotropic levels. DOCUMENTATION. — [.ter file format specification](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/terrain-type-ter-file-format-specification/); [Standard Shading Options](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/standard-shading-options-for-x-plane-10-scenery/)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: because AF with N samples reads up to log2(N) levels sharper than isotropic filtering would (prior report), the levels 0 to about 4 carry most of what is seen at grazing angles; this strengthens the case for good filters on levels 1..n and gives no route to direction-dependent prefiltering.

### Gaps
- The Wikipedia text as fetched did not contain the word "ripmap"; no source on ripmaps was opened. The statement that DDS/X-Plane cannot use them rests on the two Laminar documents listing no such option.
