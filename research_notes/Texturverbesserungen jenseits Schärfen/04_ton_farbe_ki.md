## 1. Local contrast / tone (CLAHE, local Laplacian, guided filter, "clarity", exposure fusion)

### Takeaway
All of these are spatially adaptive: the output at a pixel depends on a neighbourhood (CLAHE: per-block histograms; pyramids: up to image-sized support), so a 4096 tile processed alone will not match its neighbour unless the filter is given a border of neighbouring imagery. I found no opened source measuring a benefit on ortho/terrain imagery; the only quantitative statement on cost is a blog estimate ("under 1 ms" GPU for exposure fusion at console resolution), and the practitioners who ship these filters warn about halos, content-dependent behaviour and the "toxic HDR" look.

### Cited Findings
- CLAHE mechanism: AHE "has a tendency to overamplify noise in relatively homogeneous regions"; CLAHE limits the amplification by clipping the histogram before computing the CDF; "common values limit the resulting amplification to between 3 and 4". The efficient variant partitions the image into tiles ("64 tiles in 8 columns and 8 rows is a common choice"), computes one transformation per tile and bilinearly interpolates between tile centres; pixels near the image border are only linearly interpolated and corner pixels use the corner tile's function alone. [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Adaptive histogram equalization" (cites Pizer et al. 1987 and K. Zuiderveld, "Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization", Graphics Gems IV, 1994) — AUTHOR CLAIM (encyclopaedia summary) — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_histogram_equalization)
- OpenCV's CLAHE: default `tileGridSize` 8x8, example `clipLimit=2.0`; "If noise is there, it will be amplified"; contrast limiting clips and redistributes bins; "to remove artifacts in tile borders, bilinear interpolation is applied". [OPENED] OpenCV 4.x tutorial "Histograms - 2: Histogram Equalization" (markdown in the OpenCV repository) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/doc/py_tutorials/py_imgproc/py_histograms/py_histogram_equalization/py_histogram_equalization.markdown)
- Guided filter: edge-preserving smoothing with "linear computational complexity"; compared with the bilateral filter it avoids "gradient reversal artifacts". [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Guided filter" (cites He, Sun, Tang, "Guided Image Filtering", IEEE TPAMI 35(6), 2013) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_filter)
- Exposure fusion as local tone mapping in games: Bart Wronski (2022) describes Mertens-style exposure fusion on synthetic exposures; his earlier Gaussian-based local exposure method in God of War "caused all kinds of ugly halo artifacts"; bilateral filtering brings "gradient reversals and edge ringing". He states Lightroom's "Clarity" slider "has a very similar effect" and is "most likely a variant of 'Local Laplacian Filter'" (his guess, the algorithm is proprietary). [OPENED] B. Wronski, "Exposure Fusion – local tonemapping for real-time rendering", blog, 2022 — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION (expert practitioner) — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)
- Cost statement from the same post: "every level is just a little bit of ALU and 3 texture fetches from very low resolution textures"; "I don't have a way to profile it now ... but I believe that if implemented correctly, it should definitely be under 1ms on even previous generation consoles"; all results in the post were computed at 1/4 x 1/4 resolution and guided-upsampled. — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION (explicitly not profiled) — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)
- Risks named by the same author: "The biggest problem is its counterintuitive behavior that depends on the image frequency content. Images with strong edges will compress differently ... Even the frequency of detail (like fine scale – foliage vs medium scale like textures) matters and will affect the look"; "when pushed to the extreme, the algorithm will produce artifacts"; a naive global contrast reduction gives "the 'toxic HDR' look mixed with washed out low contrast"; simple bilinear pyramids "would alias and flicke[r] under motion". — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)
- In the 2016 post Wronski notes that for runtime local exposure the luminance used is "based only on diffuse lighting without albedo. We don't want dark/bright textures to affect the exposure" — i.e. in a renderer, local tone operators are applied to lighting, not to albedo textures. [OPENED] B. Wronski, "Localized tonemapping", blog, 2016 — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2016/08/29/localized-tonemapping/)
- Implementation of local Laplacian filters: the Halide repository ships `apps/local_laplacian/local_laplacian_generator.cpp` (default 8 pyramid levels, luminance-only remapping via a LUT, `repeat_edge` boundary condition). [OPENED] Halide source file — MEASURED (existence of code; no timing read) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/halide/Halide/main/apps/local_laplacian/local_laplacian_generator.cpp)
- Ortho4XP (offline predecessor of this pipeline) offers only *global, per-provider* colour filters, applied in `color_transform`: brightness-contrast, saturation, sharpness, blur, levels (in_min, gamma, in_max, out_min, out_max). No local/adaptive tone operator exists in that code. [OPENED] Ortho4XP `src/O4_Imagery_Utils.py` — MEASURED (code inspection) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Imagery_Utils.py)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (seams): CLAHE's interpolation scheme treats image borders differently from the interior (linear/corner cases above), and each tile's histograms see only that tile. Two adjacent 4096 tiles therefore get different transfer functions on either side of the shared edge unless (a) the block grid is defined in global Web-Mercator coordinates and (b) each tile is processed with a halo of at least one CLAHE block (512 px at an 8x8 grid) of neighbouring chunks. XEarthLayer generates tiles independently and on demand, so the halo means downloading up to 8 neighbouring tiles' edge chunks — a network cost, not a CPU cost. The same holds for pyramids (local Laplacian, exposure fusion), whose coarsest levels span the whole tile.
- OWN INFERENCE (mips): a per-4096-tile adaptive operator is also inconsistent across zoom levels (ZL16 and ZL17/18 tiles of the same ground area have different neighbourhood sizes in metres), so LOD transitions would show a tone step.
- OWN INFERENCE (albedo role): local contrast operators equalise large-scale brightness; in an albedo map this flattens real reflectance differences (dark forest vs. bright field) that the simulator's lighting should preserve, and amplifies JPEG block noise in flat regions (CLAHE noise amplification is documented above).
- OWN INFERENCE (budget): guided filter (O(N) box filters) and a single large-radius unsharp ("clarity") at reduced resolution are plausible within low tens of ms per 16 MP on one core or trivially in a wgpu pass; full local Laplacian on CPU is not plausible in the on-demand budget. No opened source gives a ms figure for 16 MP for any of them.

### Gaps
- No opened study measuring CLAHE / local Laplacian / guided-filter enhancement of orthophotos against a higher-resolution reference or in a user study for visualisation. Remote-sensing papers that use CLAHE as preprocessing for detection/segmentation exist but were not opened; that would be a different goal anyway.
- Original papers not opened (hosts unreachable or paywalled): Paris, Hasinoff, Kautz, "Local Laplacian Filters", SIGGRAPH 2011; Aubry et al., "Fast Local Laplacian Filters", ACM TOG 2014; He, Sun, Tang, "Guided Image Filtering", ECCV 2010/TPAMI 2013; Mertens et al., "Exposure Fusion", 2007; Zuiderveld 1994. Their content is cited only through the secondary sources above.
- No measured CPU timings (ms per megapixel) opened for OpenCV CLAHE or any Rust implementation. Rust availability of CLAHE (e.g. in `imageproc`) could not be verified: docs.rs page fetch returned no usable text.
- Halide repository licence not verified in this session.

## 2. Haze removal (dark channel prior and successors)

### Takeaway
Dehazing is an ill-posed estimation of a transmission map and airlight; the opened survey reports that prior-based methods such as the dark channel prior "do not fare well in general" on real-haze benchmarks (DCP 12.40 dB PSNR / 0.471 SSIM on NH-HAZE), and the 2026 systematic review treats colour bias as a core failure mode. I found no opened evidence that residual haze in commercial ortho basemap tiles is a measured problem, nor any source evaluating dehazing of textures that a renderer will haze again.

### Cited Findings
- The atmospheric scattering model makes dehazing "ill-posed ... there are multiple possible values of t(x) and A that could give the same I(x)"; the model "assumed that haze is homogeneous and occurs during the daytime"; "a limitation of [prior-based] approaches is that each prior may work wel[l]" only for particular scenes. [OPENED] G. Y. Lee et al., "Dehazing Remote Sensing and UAV Imagery: A Review of Deep Learning, Prior-based, and Hybrid Approaches", arXiv:2405.07520, 2024 (submitted to Neural Networks) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07520v1)
- DCP definition as summarised there: in haze-free non-sky pixels at least one RGB channel is dark; the dark channel is used to estimate transmission. — AUTHOR CLAIM (survey summary of He, Sun, Tang 2009) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07520v1)
- Benchmark numbers from the same survey: on NH-HAZE (2020) DCP scores PSNR 12.40 dB, SSIM 0.4710 (Table 7); "The prior-based approaches do not fare well in general, compared to the rest of approaches"; on DENSE-HAZE the best method reaches only 16.95 dB / 0.5930 and "the visual outputs in this case are not very pleasing". DCP rows in the survey's other tables range from 10.06 to 19.13 dB (I did not map each row to its dataset). — MEASURED (compiled by survey authors from the literature) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07520v1)
- The survey notes that most dehazing work is tested on synthetic-haze benchmarks (e.g. RESIDE with 110,500 synthetic hazy images) and that "most dehazing works have focused more on the haze removal step directly, rather than on the removal of haze artifacts that can occur after the haze is removed. Because detail loss may be unavoidable during a standard dehazing operation ...". — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07520v1)
- 2026 systematic review (abstract): benchmarks 30+ methods on five datasets with 12 metrics; "Transformer- and diffusion-based models improve SSIM by 12%~18% and reduce perceptual errors by 20%~35% on average"; "models with explicit transmission or airlight constraints reduce color bias by up to 27%"; open challenges include "lightweight deployment". [ABSTRACT OPENED] H. Zhou, X. Liu et al., "Remote Sensing Image Dehazing: A Systematic Review of Progress, Challenges, and Prospects", arXiv:2603.20289, 2026 (82 pages) — MEASURED per abstract (full tables not read) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20289)
- Radiometric degradation is acknowledged as a general property of remote-sensing imagery: degradations "stem from ... atmospheric scattering effects (e.g., haze, turbulence ...), sensor-specific imperfections ..., radiometric inconsistencies, temporal variations across acquisition cycles (e.g., seasonal illumination changes and phenological variations), and preprocessing artifacts". [OPENED] Qi et al., "Advancing Image Super-resolution Techniques in Remote Sensing: A Comprehensive Survey", arXiv:2505.23248v3, 2025 (ISPRS J. per search listing) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23248v3)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE ("double haze" argument): physically, a texture with residual airlight is albedo·t + A·(1−t); the simulator then applies its own distance haze, so residual haze in the texture lowers contrast twice. The argument is sound in principle, but it only matters if tiles contain noticeable haze, and I have no measurement of that. Provider mosaics are produced from selected, already corrected acquisitions; the project's own finding (luma 1.28x, saturation 0.79x between acquisitions) is consistent with acquisition differences that include atmosphere, but does not isolate haze.
- OWN INFERENCE (seams): DCP estimates one airlight per image and a transmission map from a patch-minimum; per-tile estimation would give different airlight per tile → visible tile steps. A dehazing step would have to use a globally fixed or very low-frequency (lower-zoom) airlight/transmission estimate, which turns it into the low-frequency colour matching of section 4.
- OWN INFERENCE (DCP validity): the dark-channel assumption needs dark pixels (shadows, dark vegetation) in every patch; snow, desert, salt flats and water violate it, so DCP would darken and colour-shift exactly the bright homogeneous terrains. The opened survey supports the general statement that each prior fails on some scene types but does not give this specific case.
- OWN INFERENCE (cost): DCP = min-filter + guided-filter refinement + per-pixel division; computed on a 1/4-resolution copy this is cheap (a few ms per 16 MP is plausible), but I have no opened timing.

### Gaps
- He, Sun, Tang, "Single Image Haze Removal Using Dark Channel Prior" (CVPR 2009 / TPAMI 2011) not opened (no arXiv version; author page unreachable; the Wikipedia title I tried does not exist).
- No opened measurement of how often/how strongly Bing/Google/ArcGIS/Apple tiles at ~2.4 m/px contain residual haze.
- No opened source on dehazing textures prior to re-rendering with atmospheric scattering (games/simulators).
- Unverified (snippet only): J. Liu et al., "A Review of Remote Sensing Image Dehazing", Sensors 21(11):3926, 2021 — classifies methods into image enhancement, physical dehazing, data-driven (search snippet).

## 3. De-lighting / shadow reduction for use as albedo

### Takeaway
The literature confirms the premise (photographic textures embed sun and shadow and look wrong under synthetic lighting), but the published methods need scene geometry or run in the order of a second per (small) image, and the deep-learning shadow literature reports limited cross-dataset generalisation. Nothing opened shows a real-time, tile-consistent de-lighting method for 2.4 m/px ortho tiles; I could not open any first-party statement from Asobo/Blackshark/Microsoft about colour or shadow processing of Bing imagery.

### Cited Findings
- Premise, from an aerial-photogrammetry paper: textures from standard pipelines "intrinsically embedded the spatially and temporally variant environmental lighting information, such as the sun illumination, direction ... making such models less realistic when used in 3D rendering under synthetic lightings"; their albedo recovery uses an outdoor image-formation model and "the typical photogrammetric products as an initial approximation of the geometry". [ABSTRACT OPENED] S. Song, R. Qin, "A Novel Intrinsic Image Decomposition Method to Recover Albedo for Aerial Images in Photogrammetry Processing", arXiv:2204.04142 ("to be published in ISPRS Congress 2022") — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.04142)
- Remote-sensing shadow removal state of the art (2026): SARU's training-free removal step "attains an average processing speed of approximately 1.3 s, which is over 10 times faster than the SOTA MAOSD"; it first needs a detected shadow mask and restores illumination "by transferring properties from adjacent non-shadow regions within the single input image". [ABSTRACT OPENED] Z.-Y. Bo, W. Lu, H. Chen, S.-B. Chen et al., "SARU: A Shadow-Aware and Removal Unified Framework for Remote Sensing Images with New Benchmarks", arXiv:2604.25432, 2026 ("Accepted by ISPRS") — MEASURED per abstract (image size and hardware not stated in the abstract) — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25432)
- General deep shadow removal: a unified benchmark found "inconsistencies in prior reports, strong dependence on model design and resolution, and limited cross-dataset generalization due to dataset bias". [ABSTRACT OPENED] "Unveiling Deep Shadows: A Survey and Benchmark on Image and Video Shadow Detection, Removal, and Generation in the Deep Learning Era", arXiv:2409.02108 (accepted by IJCV) — MEASURED per abstract — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02108)
- Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020): the game "simulates the topography of the Earth using data from Bing Maps", uses Azure "to provide over two petabytes of world map data", and "Microsoft partnered with Blackshark.ai". The article says nothing about colour correction, cloud or shadow removal of the aerial imagery. [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Microsoft Flight Simulator (2020 video game)" — secondary summary — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Flight_Simulator_(2020_video_game))
- X-Plane's own documentation equates the base texture with albedo: "as far as we're concerned, 'albedo' is just another name for 'daylight texture'". [OPENED] Laminar Research, "Using Decals to Add Detail To Scenery", developer.x-plane.com, last updated 30 May 2014 — VENDOR CLAIM — [Source](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: at 2.4 m/px most cast shadows (buildings, trees) are a few pixels wide; mask-based shadow removal built for 0.1–0.5 m aerial images has little to work with, and removing terrain-scale shading (north slopes) needs a DEM and sun position per acquisition, which tiles do not carry (Bing imagery lacks timestamps according to a search snippet, unverified).
- OWN INFERENCE: 1.3 s per image (SARU, unknown size) is two orders of magnitude outside the on-demand budget; even for prefetch it is hallucination-prone inpainting. De-lighting is not plausible for XEarthLayer.
- OWN INFERENCE: a partial, cheap substitute is not removal but *consistency*: keeping baked shading but equalising low-frequency colour between acquisitions (section 4).

## 4. Colour consistency across acquisitions and providers

### Takeaway
The problem XEarthLayer measured (different acquisitions → different luma/saturation) is the classical "radiometric normalisation / colour consistency" problem of mosaicking, and the textbook tools (histogram matching, Reinhard-style statistics transfer) are explicitly meant for images of the same location under different sensor/atmosphere conditions. I found no opened publication that does exactly "match each tile's low-frequency colour to the provider's lower-zoom imagery or to a global reference (Sentinel-2 cloudless, Blue Marble)"; the idea is an OWN INFERENCE that is cheap and structurally seam-free if the reference is sampled continuously, but its benefit is unmeasured.

### Cited Findings
- Histogram matching "can be used to normalize two images, when the images were acquired at the same local illumination (such as shadows) over the same location, but by different sensors, atmospheric conditions or global illumination". [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Histogram matching" (cites Gonzalez & Woods, Digital Image Processing, 3rd ed.) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histogram_matching)
- Colour transfer taxonomy: algorithms either "employ the statistics of the colors of two images" or "rely on a given pixel correspondence"; histogram matching "is a classic algorithm for color transfer, but it can suffer from the problem that it is too precise so that it copies very particular color quirks from the target image, rather than the general color characteristics"; Reinhard et al. is called "the seminal paper". [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Image color transfer" — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image_color_transfer)
- Mosaic colour consistency, problem statement: "Due to different seasons, illumination, and atmospheric conditions, the photometric of the acquired image varies greatly, which leads to obvious stitching seams at the edges of the mosaic image. Traditional methods can be divided into two categories, one is absolute radiation correction and the other is relative radiation normalization." Their NeRF-based fix is evaluated only visually ("excellent visual effect and smooth color transition at the edges"). [ABSTRACT OPENED] Z. Zuo, Y. Li, T. Zhang, "A Nerf-Based Color Consistency Method for Remote Sensing Images", arXiv:2411.05557, IGARSS 2023, 4 pages — AUTHOR CLAIM, no numbers in abstract — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05557)
- Pansharpening (the closest established "take colour from the low-resolution source, detail from the high-resolution source" technique): "Low-res color bands + High-res grayscale band = High-res color image"; "Google Maps and nearly every map creating company use this technique to increase image quality". [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Pansharpening" (flagged "refimprove") — AUTHOR CLAIM, weakly sourced — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pansharpening)
- Prior practice in this exact ecosystem: Ortho4XP lets the user attach a fixed colour filter per provider/combined provider (brightness-contrast, saturation, levels with gamma, sharpness, blur) — i.e. manual global correction per source, chosen offline by a human; no automatic matching. [OPENED] Ortho4XP `src/O4_Imagery_Utils.py` — MEASURED (code inspection) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Imagery_Utils.py)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (design): "low-frequency colour transfer to a reference": compute tile mean/variance (Reinhard) or a coarse grid of gains per channel from a heavily downsampled copy (e.g. 64x64 from the 16 MP tile) against a reference raster covering the same ground, then apply gain/offset fields bilinearly interpolated. If the *reference* is spatially continuous (one global raster, or the provider's own zoom ~8–10 imagery) and the correction field is a function of ground position only (grid anchored in Web Mercator, not in the tile), adjacent tiles agree at their shared edge by construction — unlike per-tile statistics, which are exactly the tile-step failure mode.
- OWN INFERENCE (cost): statistics on a 64x64 or 256x256 downsample are negligible; applying a per-pixel gain+offset per channel to 16 MP is a single pass (a few ms on one core with SIMD, trivial in the existing wgpu path). No opened source gives a timing; the arithmetic is one multiply-add per channel per pixel.
- OWN INFERENCE (limits): (a) the provider's own low zoom is built from the same mixed acquisitions, so it inherits the patchwork at a coarser scale; only an independent, homogeneous reference removes it; (b) an independent reference has its own season/year and ~10–500 m resolution, so colour is pulled towards that season; (c) acquisition boundaries inside a tile are sharp edges, which a low-frequency field can only soften over its grid spacing, not remove; (d) the reference needs an extra download or a shipped dataset and a licence check (not researched). This is the only candidate in this report that addresses the cause the project identified (acquisition differences) rather than a symptom, but there is no measured evidence of benefit.
- OWN INFERENCE: full histogram matching per tile is contra-indicated: it is "too precise" (source above) and content-dependent — a tile that is 80% forest matched to a reference histogram shifts differently from its 80%-field neighbour.

- No arXiv survey on mosaic colour balancing/dodging found; the relevant literature sits in ISPRS/IEEE/MDPI journals that were not opened.
- Reinhard, Ashikhmin, Gooch, Shirley, "Color Transfer between Images", IEEE CG&A 2001 not opened (IEEE).
- No opened source for Sentinel-2 cloudless (EOX) or NASA Blue Marble licences/resolution; no opened example of "tone matching to basemap" in a streaming ortho product.

## 5. Debanding / dithering and film-grain / noise synthesis

### Takeaway
Dithering is a well-founded fix for quantisation banding, and decoder-side grain synthesis is deployed at scale in video (AV1), but the claim "added noise increases perceived sharpness" could not be verified from any opened source here. X-Plane already has the correct mechanism for texture-fixed high-frequency detail — decals that modulate the albedo and fade to "no effect" with distance — and Ortho4XP exposes it as an option whose own hint calls it helpful at very low altitude and "slightly distracting" higher up.

### Cited Findings
- Dithering definition and rationale: "intentional / deliberate adding of some noise to signal to prevent large-scale / low resolution errors that come from quantization or undersampling"; white-noise dither can increase maximum error while removing low-frequency error; error at the upper end of perceivable spatial frequency "will be much less visible", motivating blue noise. [OPENED] B. Wronski, "Dithering part one – simple quantization", blog, 2016 — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION (expert, with worked plots) — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2016/10/30/dithering-part-one-simple-quantization/)
- Film grain synthesis in codecs: "some video codecs, notably AV1, include film grain synthesis, where the film grain is removed during encoding and replaced with parameters ... during playback the decoder uses these parameters to resynthesize the film grain"; Netflix announced in July 2025 streaming with AV1 FGS at scale; "in its tests the grain-synthesized streams needed lower bitrates and caused about 10 per cent fewer rebuffering events". [OPENED] Wikipedia, "Film grain" (citing TVBEurope, 3 July 2025) — VENDOR CLAIM relayed by encyclopaedia; this is evidence for bitrate saving, not for perceived sharpness — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_grain)
- X-Plane decals: "Decals are overlay textures that sit on top of a regular albedo (day-time) texture to add high-frequency, high-res detail"; they "work by modulating the textures they overlay"; "when viewed from far away, the entire decal blends together into a constant color/grayscale. The decal shader ensures that ... the net result is the same as your underlying albedo ... as you move away from the decal, the decal blurs into 'no effect'"; greyscale decals use "a blending mode similar to Photoshop's 'hard light' mode"; decals are allowed in `.ter`, `.pol`, `.net` files; example `DECAL 25.0 ...` repeats every 60 m on a 1500 m texture. [OPENED] Laminar Research, "Using Decals to Add Detail To Scenery", developer.x-plane.com, 2014 — VENDOR CLAIM / documentation — [Source](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)
- Ortho4XP option `use_decal_on_terrain` (default False): "Terrain files for all but water triangles will contain the maquify_1_green_key.dcl decal directive. The effect is noticeable at very low altitude and helps to overcome the orthophoto blur at such levels. Can be slightly distracting at higher altitude." [OPENED] Ortho4XP `src/O4_Config_Utils.py` — AUTHOR CLAIM (tool author's hint text) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (noise baked into the DDS): noise baked at mip 0 (a) averages away in the linear-light mip chain by construction, so it only exists where mip 0 is displayed, i.e. where the texture is magnified or near 1:1 — exactly where the decal mechanism works too; (b) costs BC1 quality: every 4x4 block has two endpoints and 4 colours on a line, so per-pixel RGB noise competes with real detail for the block's palette (standard BC1 property; the companion report on encoding should carry the measured numbers); (c) is fixed in texture space, so it does not shimmer by itself, but at minification without sufficient anisotropic filtering any high-frequency content aliases — again the mip chain removes it first.
- OWN INFERENCE: since `.ter` files and decals are authored by the scenery package (Ortho4XP / the XEarthLayer publisher), not by the streamed DDS, synthetic fine detail belongs in the terrain definition (decal directive), where the simulator applies it at screen-appropriate frequency and fades it, rather than in the 2.4 m/px texture. That is a packaging decision, zero runtime cost in XEarthLayer.
- OWN INFERENCE: debanding of 8-bit JPEG gradients before BC1 has no obvious target: BC1's RGB565 endpoints quantise far more coarsely than the source; endpoint dithering is an encoder question, not an image-enhancement one.

### Gaps
- Unverified (snippet only), could not open SPIE/IS&T/J-STAGE: Kurihara, Aoki, Kobayashi, "Analysis of sharpness increase by image noise", Proc. SPIE 7240 (HVEI 2009) and J. Imaging Sci. Technol. 55(3), 2011 — snippets say the effect is conditional: sharpness mostly decreased with noise for high-frequency stimuli, increased at a certain noise level for lower-frequency/texture stimuli, and decreased when noise was applied to edge regions; a 2012 ITE paper (Wan, Imaizumi, Aoki, Kobayashi) reportedly found noise raised sharpness only for a texture image without clear edges. If these snippets are accurate, they argue *against* a general "noise sharpens" rule and say nothing about terrain textures under a renderer.
- Johnson & Fairchild, "Sharpness Rules" (CIC 2000) and Kayargadde & Martens (JOSA A 1996) not found/opened.
- No opened source on noise injection for game terrain albedo, or on how X-Plane 12's own terrain shader applies detail noise to orthophoto `.ter` files by default.

## 6. Learned super-resolution / restoration

### Takeaway
The numbers that exist are for other content and other budgets: the heavy models (SwinIR: 0.539 s for a 256x256 input on an RTX 2080 Ti; Real-ESRGAN: authors list twisted lines, GAN artefacts and amplification of out-of-distribution degradations; its tiled executable "may introduce block inconsistency") are orders of magnitude too slow and hallucinate, while the real-time class (ESPCN-like, NTIRE/AIS challenge networks: 1–30 ms on RTX 3090/4090 for a 0.5–2 MP *input*) improves on Lanczos/bicubic by only about 0.07–0.4 dB PSNR. No opened source shows a benefit for ~2.4 m/px ortho tiles used as terrain textures, and remote-sensing SR research itself states that models trained on synthetic degradations have limited real-world applicability.

### Cited Findings

- Real-ESRGAN: trained "with pure synthetic data" using a high-order degradation model; "superior visual performance" (no PSNR claim in the abstract). ### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (heavy models, cost): a 4096x4096 tile is 256 patches of 256x256. At SwinIR's measured 0.539 s per 256x256 input on an RTX 2080 Ti, one tile takes about 138 s; RCAN about 46 s. Real-ESRGAN x4plus is an RRDB network of the same weight class (no timing opened). These are 3–4 orders of magnitude over the on-demand budget and far too slow even for prefetch (thousands of tiles per flight), and they would occupy the GPU X-Plane renders with.
- OWN INFERENCE (fast models, cost): the challenge figures are for a 0.5 MP (540p) or 2 MP (1080p) input on an RTX 3090/4090. A 16 MP tile as *input* is 8x a 1080p frame: RTSRN-class ≈ 8 x 29.9 ms ≈ 240 ms FP16 on a 3090 (≈ 90 ms with TensorRT); the smallest AIS networks (≈1 ms per 0.52 MP) ≈ 30 ms per 16 MP on a 4090-class GPU. So only the 3-layer-convolution class approaches the budget, and only on a high-end GPU that is simultaneously rendering the simulator.
- OWN INFERENCE (what it would buy): that fastest class improves on Lanczos by 0.07–0.4 dB PSNR on its own benchmark — a fidelity gain that is real but visually marginal, measured on digital art/games/photos, not orthophotos. Larger perceptual gains come only from GAN/diffusion models, whose "detail" is hallucinated (authors' own limitation list above).
- OWN INFERENCE (structural mismatch): SR increases pixel count. The tile is fixed at 4096² for the DDS. Either (a) upscale ZL16 chunks to stand in for ZL17 (a 4x larger texture per ground area, 4x VRAM and BC1 encode time — and the real ZL17 imagery is usually one request away, with true rather than invented detail), or (b) upscale then downsample back, which reduces SR to a learned denoise/deblock filter at 4096². The project's finding that unsharp masking does not approach the higher-zoom reference is the relevant precedent: any SR candidate should be tested against the provider's real next zoom level with the same metric before anything else.
- OWN INFERENCE (consistency): CNN inference is deterministic, so a given tile is temporally stable; the risks are spatial: (1) tile seams unless each tile is inferred with a halo of neighbouring chunks at least the receptive-field wide (the Real-ESRGAN README documents exactly this "block inconsistency"); (2) LOD inconsistency — hallucinated structures at ZL16 will not match the real structures that appear when the ZL17/18 tile loads nearer the aircraft, producing a visible content pop rather than a sharpness pop; (3) domain shift — none of the opened models was trained on 2.4 m/px JPEG ortho tiles.
- OWN INFERENCE (integration): the existing wgpu compute path could run a 3–5 layer convolution network as hand-written WGSL, but there is no maintained wgpu ONNX runtime (`wonnx` unmaintained since 2023 and lacking `DepthToSpace`); `ort` works but adds the ONNX Runtime native library and its GPU execution providers to a binary that currently links none, on Linux and macOS; `candle`/`burn`/`tract` are pure-Rust alternatives whose performance for this workload I did not verify.

### Gaps
- NTIRE 2023 challenge report PDF and RT4KSR paper PDF (openaccess.thecvf.com) could not be opened; only the repositories' READMEs were read. Per-team runtimes of NTIRE 2023 are therefore missing.
- No CPU inference timings opened for any SR model (16 MP on CPU is presumably seconds to minutes; no source).
- Model file sizes for Real-ESRGAN weights not verified (release assets not accessible).
- No opened satellite-specific SR model evaluated on Bing/Google-type RGB basemap tiles; no opened hallucination study for satellite SR (the opened survey does not use the word).
- Flight-sim community practice of AI-upscaled ortho (forum posts on x-plane.org, AVSIM, MSFS forums, Reddit) could not be opened — no verified statement in either direction.
- Unverified (snippet only): "Deep learning for single-image super-resolution in remote sensing: A review" (2026 preprint on a University of Wisconsin site) listing training-data quality and generalisation as open challenges; NTIRE 2025 Efficient SR report (arXiv:2504.10686) "average runtime of the top three solutions ... below 10 ms"; NTIRE 2026 Efficient SR (arXiv:2604.03198) 7.65 ms on RTX A6000 for one entry — these are x4 efficient-SR tracks on small inputs, not 4K output.

## 7. Water handling (sun glint, waves baked into orthophotos)

### Takeaway
Confirmed from Ortho4XP source: water is handled by the scenery package (mesh water triangles, alpha masks along the coast, a constant transparency for inland water, optional blur of sea imagery), not by the texture content — so it is not an image-enhancement task for XEarthLayer's tile pipeline.

### Cited Findings
- Masks: `mask_zl` — "The zoomlevel at which the (sea) water masks are built. Masks are used for alpha channel"; `masks_width` — "Maximum extent of the masks perpendicularly to the coastline"; `masking_mode` with three algorithms (sand, rocks, 3steps); `use_masks_for_inland` — masks for lakes/rivers "instead of the default constant transparency level determined by ratio_water"; `imprint_masks_to_dds` — "Will apply masking directly to dds textures ... rather than using external png files. This doubles the file size of masked textures (dxt5 vs dxt1)". [OPENED] Ortho4XP `src/O4_Config_Utils.py` — MEASURED (code/hint text) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)
- `ratio_water`: "Inland water rendering is made of two layers: one bottom layer of 'X-Plane water' and one overlay layer of orthophoto with constant level of transparency applied ... At zero, the orthophoto is fully opaque ...; at 1 the orthophoto is fully transparent and only the X-Plane water is seen." `overlay_lod`: distance until which "orthophotos over water" overlays are drawn. — same file — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)
- Baked wave/reflection patterns are acknowledged and handled by blurring: for mask layers in combined providers a blur radius "allows to smoothen some sea imageries where the wave or reflection pattern was too much present". — same file — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)
- Mask construction: `O4_Mask_Utils.py` records water triangles from the mesh ("Record water tris form mesh"), builds a water pre-mask, optionally combines a DEM-based mask, blurs it, and keeps land at 255. [OPENED] Ortho4XP `src/O4_Mask_Utils.py` — MEASURED (code inspection) — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Mask_Utils.py)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE: because the alpha mask / water triangle decides where the photo is visible at all, glint and waves in the RGB matter only in the semi-transparent shoreline band; Ortho4XP's own remedy there is a blur, i.e. *less* detail. Nothing here calls for enhancement in the tile generator.

### Gaps
- Whether XEarthLayer-published packages use `imprint_masks_to_dds` (alpha inside the streamed DDS, i.e. BC3) or external mask PNGs was not checked in this task; if alpha were imprinted, any colour processing would have to leave alpha untouched.

## 8. Counter-evidence: does enhancing albedo/terrain imagery harm realism or consistency?

### Takeaway
I found no controlled study either way for terrain textures. What exists is (a) expert/practitioner warnings that local tone operators produce halos, content-dependent results and an over-processed look, and are applied to lighting rather than albedo in renderers; (b) authors' own limitation statements for GAN SR and dehazing (hallucination, artefact amplification, colour bias); (c) the X-Plane documentation's design principle that added detail must fade to "no effect" at distance; and (d) the project's own negative results. The argument "provider imagery is already colour-balanced" is plausible but I could not open a provider statement that documents it.

### Cited Findings
- Local tone mapping: "counterintuitive behavior that depends on the image frequency content"; extreme settings give "dark halos here and there, weird washouts"; naive contrast compression "resembles a lot of the 'toxic HDR' look". [OPENED] Wronski 2022 — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2022/02/28/exposure-fusion-local-tonemapping-for-real-time-rendering/)
- Renderer practice: local exposure is driven by lighting "without albedo. We don't want dark/bright textures to affect the exposure". [OPENED] Wronski 2016 — BLOG OR FORUM OPINION — [Source](https://bartwronski.com/2016/08/29/localized-tonemapping/)
- Histogram-based adaptive contrast amplifies noise in homogeneous regions (the reason CLAHE exists). [OPENED] Wikipedia AHE; OpenCV tutorial — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_histogram_equalization)
- GAN SR: "twisted lines", "unpleasant artifacts caused by GAN training", may "amplify" out-of-distribution artefacts; tiled inference "may introduce block inconsistency". [OPENED] Real-ESRGAN paper §4.4 and README — AUTHOR CLAIM — [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.10833), [README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN/master/README.md)
- Dehazing: prior-based methods "do not fare well in general"; colour bias is a tracked failure mode that physics constraints only reduce ("by up to 27%"). [OPENED] arXiv:2405.07520; [ABSTRACT OPENED] arXiv:2603.20289 — MEASURED/AUTHOR CLAIM — [2405.07520](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.07520v1), [2603.20289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20289)
- X-Plane design principle for added detail: the decal shader guarantees that at distance "the net result is the same as your underlying albedo". [OPENED] developer.x-plane.com decal article — VENDOR CLAIM — [Source](https://developer.x-plane.com/article/using-decals-to-add-detail-to-scenery/)
- Even the ortho tool's own optional detail overlay is described as "slightly distracting at higher altitude". [OPENED] Ortho4XP `O4_Config_Utils.py` — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/oscarpilote/Ortho4XP/master/src/O4_Config_Utils.py)
- Providers do process imagery before serving: pansharpening is used by "Google Maps and nearly every map creating company". [OPENED] Wikipedia "Pansharpening" (weakly sourced) — AUTHOR CLAIM — [Source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pansharpening)

### Inferences
- OWN INFERENCE (summary table; "evidence of benefit" refers to opened sources and to this use case):

|---|---|---|---|---|

- OWN INFERENCE: the common pattern is that every *spatially adaptive* method needs context beyond the tile to be seam-free, and every method that adds *content* (dehaze, de-light, GAN SR, noise) conflicts with the texture's role as input to the simulator's own lighting, haze and LOD chain. The methods that survive both constraints are position-anchored, low-frequency, content-preserving corrections (section 4) and detail added by the simulator itself (decals, section 5).
